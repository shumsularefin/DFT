<Section id="why" heading="Why test before you relax">

ENCUT (how many plane waves) and the k-mesh (how finely the Brillouin zone is sampled) control the accuracy
of every later run. Energy differences between structures and the quantities derived from them converge
much faster than the total energy itself, so judge convergence on those differences, not on the absolute
total energy<Cite id="S-042" />.

The method, from the Workflow Handbook<Cite id="S-042" />:

- **Test on the unrelaxed structure** with static (single-point) runs. You do not need the relaxed cell yet.
- **Change one setting at a time.** Scan ENCUT with the k-mesh fixed, then scan the k-mesh with ENCUT
  fixed at the value you chose.
- **Pick the smallest setting** at which the energy changes by less than 1 meV/atom from the next step up.
- **For stress-based quantities** (elastic constants, the equation of state), also watch the pressure.

</Section>

<Section id="inputs" heading="The inputs">

Both scans use the same static INCAR. The scripts below rewrite only its ENCUT line:

<PresetIncar preset="convergence" caption="Static INCAR for the scans" />

Use the POSCAR and POTCAR from [Prepare the inputs](/workflow/prepare-inputs/). For the ENCUT scan, keep
KPOINTS fixed at a reasonable mesh, for example VASPkit 102 at 0.04.

</Section>

<Section id="encut" heading="Scan ENCUT">

The script makes one folder per cutoff (400 to 700 eV in steps of 50), runs VASP in each, and writes the
cutoff and the "energy without entropy" to `encut.dat`:

<VaspFile
  name="shell"
  code={`#!/bin/bash
# static INCAR with an ENCUT line; KPOINTS fixed at a reasonable mesh
NP=32
for e in 400 450 500 550 600 650 700; do
  d=encut_$e; mkdir -p $d; cp POSCAR POTCAR KPOINTS $d/
  sed "s/^ *ENCUT.*/ENCUT = $e/" INCAR > $d/INCAR
  (cd $d && mpirun -np $NP vasp_std > vasp.out)
  E=$(grep "energy  without entropy" $d/OUTCAR | tail -1 | awk '{print $NF}')
  echo "$e $E" >> encut.dat
done
# divide by number of atoms and look at successive differences`}
/>

Save it as `encut_test.sh` in `00_conv/`. `NP=32` is the number of MPI ranks: set it to the cores you have.
When it finishes, divide each energy by the number of atoms in the cell and look at the difference between
each cutoff and the next one up. Keep the smallest cutoff whose energy per atom differs from the next
cutoff up by less than 1 meV/atom.

</Section>

<Section id="kpoints" heading="Scan the k-mesh">

Now fix ENCUT at the converged value in the INCAR. The script lets VASPkit build each mesh (task 102,
Gamma-centred, at resolutions 0.06 down to 0.02) and writes the resolution, the mesh and the energy to
`kpt.dat`:

<VaspFile
  name="shell"
  code={`#!/bin/bash
# ENCUT fixed at the converged value; VASPkit builds each mesh
for r in 0.06 0.05 0.04 0.03 0.025 0.02; do
  d=kpt_$r; mkdir -p $d; cp POSCAR POTCAR INCAR $d/
  (cd $d && printf "102\\n2\\n$r\\n" | vaspkit > /dev/null && mpirun -np 32 vasp_std > vasp.out)
  E=$(grep "energy  without entropy" $d/OUTCAR | tail -1 | awk '{print $NF}')
  echo "$r $(sed -n 4p $d/KPOINTS) $E" >> kpt.dat
done`}
/>

Save it as `kpt_test.sh`. The `printf` line types the answers to the 102 menu: 2 picks a Gamma-centred
mesh, then the resolution. `sed -n 4p` copies the mesh line of each KPOINTS into `kpt.dat`, so you can see
which mesh each resolution gave. Read the result the same way as the ENCUT scan: energy per atom,
differences to the next step, 1 meV/atom.

</Section>

<Section id="read" heading="Reading the result">

Paste `encut.dat` into the [Convergence plotter](/tools/convergence/), choose "ENCUT series", give the
number of atoms and pick "energy without entropy". It plots the energy per atom against the cutoff and
marks where the change drops below your threshold. For `kpt.dat`, paste only the resolution and the energy
(the first and last columns, for example `awk '{print $1, $NF}' kpt.dat`) and choose "k-mesh by
resolution".

Rules of thumb from the Workflow Handbook<Cite id="S-042" />:

- **Insulators** converge with coarse meshes (resolution 0.04). **Metals** need 0.02 or finer, plus a
  smearing test.
- Use Gamma-centred meshes for hexagonal and trigonal cells.
- **Convergence carries over.** A supercell N times larger needs about N times fewer k-points in each
  direction.
- **Report both settings,** the cutoff and the k-mesh (or its resolution), in your methods section.

</Section>
