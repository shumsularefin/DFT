<Section id="why" heading="What VASP does in this step">

With IBRION = 6 and ISIF ≥ 3, VASP applies small positive and negative strains to the lattice, one for
each of the six independent strain components. It also displaces the ions, then computes how the stress
responds. You get two parts: the clamped-ion elastic tensor (ions held fixed) and a correction from the
internal relaxation of the ions. Their sum is printed in OUTCAR as TOTAL ELASTIC MODULI<Cite id="S-042" />.

The stress is very sensitive to the plane-wave basis. That is why a high cutoff and a fully relaxed
cell are essential for this step.

</Section>

<Section id="before" heading="Before you run">

- **Re-relax at the elastic settings.** Relax with exactly the ENCUT, PREC and k-mesh of the elastic
  run. Then `grep "external pressure" OUTCAR | tail -1` should be within about ±1 kB. A larger leftover
  pressure means the cell is not at its minimum, and the Cij come out wrong.
- **Use a denser k-mesh than for the relaxation**, especially for metals. The Cij converge slowly with
  k-points.
- **Use the conventional cell** when its axes line up with the crystal axes you want the Cij in (cubic,
  for example). Then VASPkit's analysis for your crystal system applies cleanly. VASPkit 603
  (conventional cell) or 604 (standardized crystal cell) writes the cell to use for elastic
  constants<Cite id="S-020" />.

The relaxation itself is described in [Relax the structure](/workflow/relaxation/).

</Section>

<Section id="inputs" heading="The inputs">

Start from VASPkit 101 → DC and edit it to match this file:

<PresetIncar preset="elastic" caption="INCAR for the elastic run (101 → DC, edited)" />

The author's file ends with the line `# NCORE = 4`, commented out with the note that IBRION = 6 runs
without band parallelization. Keep NCORE out of your INCAR for this run.

| Tag    | Setting | Reason                                                                |
| ------ | ------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| ENCUT  | 620     | At least 1.3 × ENMAX, and the same value as the re-relaxation.        |
| EDIFF  | 1E-8    | 1E-7 is the minimum. The stress is only as good as the SCF behind it. |
| IBRION | 6       | Finite differences that use the crystal symmetry.                     |
| ISIF   | 3       | Required for VASP to compute the elastic tensor.                      |
| NFREE  | 4       | Displacements per direction: 2 is cheaper, 4 is more accurate.        |
| POTIM  | 0.015   | The size of each displacement and strain step.                        |
| ISPIN  | 2       | Only if the material is magnetic, with one MAGMOM value per atom.     |

</Section>

<Section id="run" heading="Run and extract">

Run VASP as usual, then read the tensor:

<VaspFile
  name="shell"
  code={`grep -A 9 "TOTAL ELASTIC MODULI" OUTCAR   # kBar; divide by 10 for GPa
printf "203\\n" | vaspkit                  # reads TOTAL ELASTIC MODULI from OUTCAR`}
/>

The first line prints the matrix from OUTCAR in kBar (divide by 10 for GPa). The second feeds the menu
choice 203 to VASPkit, which does the full analysis.

<Callout variant="mistake" title="The ordering trap">

OUTCAR orders the rows and columns as XX YY ZZ XY YZ ZX. Voigt notation orders them 11 22 33 23 13 12,
that is YZ ZX XY for the last three. So VASP's 4th, 5th and 6th entries are Voigt 6, 4 and 5. VASPkit 203
converts this for you. If you parse OUTCAR yourself, swap them yourself.

</Callout>

</Section>

<Section id="read" heading="What VASPkit 203 reports">

203 analyses the OUTCAR of VASP's IBRION = 6 run, which is the route of this protocol<Cite id="S-020" />.
It prints:

- the Cij (in GPa) and the compliance matrix Sij;
- the bulk modulus B, Young's modulus E, shear modulus G and Poisson ratio ν as Voigt, Reuss and Hill
  averages, plus their directional minimum and maximum (anisotropy);
- the P-wave modulus and the Pugh ratio;
- the Vickers hardness by two models (Chen and Tian);
- the Cauchy pressure and the Kleinman parameter;
- the universal and the Chung–Buessem anisotropy indices;
- the longitudinal, transverse and average sound velocities, and the Debye temperature;
- the six eigenvalues of the stiffness matrix and the Born stability criteria.

It also writes the tensor to the file `ELASTIC_TENSOR`. For the Cauchy pressure, read the sign as in
the indicator table below: positive means metallic and ductile<Cite id="S-042" />.

</Section>

<Section id="stability" heading="Is the crystal stable? (Born criteria)">

The general test in the first row works for any lattice. The other rows are the conditions the author
gives for three crystal systems<Cite id="S-072" />.

| Lattice        | Conditions                                                                               |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Any            | All six eigenvalues of the 6×6 C matrix are positive (general, necessary and sufficient) |
| Cubic          | `C₁₁ − C₁₂ > 0`; `C₁₁ + 2C₁₂ > 0`; `C₄₄ > 0`                                             |
| Hexagonal      | `C₁₁ > \|C₁₂\|`; `2C₁₃² < C₃₃(C₁₁ + C₁₂)`; `C₄₄ > 0`; `C₆₆ = (C₁₁ − C₁₂)/2 > 0`          |
| Tetragonal (I) | `C₁₁ > \|C₁₂\|`; `2C₁₃² < C₃₃(C₁₁ + C₁₂)`; `C₄₄ > 0`; `C₆₆ > 0`                          |

If one eigenvalue is negative, the structure is mechanically unstable as given. VASPkit 203 prints the
six eigenvalues and checks the criteria for you.

</Section>

<Section id="moduli" heading="Derived moduli (Voigt, Reuss, Hill)">

VASPkit 203 averages the Cij in three ways: Voigt (from the Cij), Reuss (from the compliance Sij) and
Hill, the mean of the two<Cite id="S-076" />. These are the formulas behind the 203 numbers, as the
author gives them<Cite id="S-042" />:

- `B_V = [(C11+C22+C33) + 2(C12+C13+C23)] / 9`
- `G_V = [(C11+C22+C33) − (C12+C13+C23) + 3(C44+C55+C66)] / 15`
- `B_R = 1 / [(S11+S22+S33) + 2(S12+S13+S23)]`, where `S = C⁻¹` (the compliance matrix)
- `G_R = 15 / [4(S11+S22+S33) − 4(S12+S13+S23) + 3(S44+S55+S66)]`
- `B_H = (B_V + B_R)/2` and `G_H = (G_V + G_R)/2`
- `E = 9BG / (3B + G)` and `ν = (3B − 2G) / [2(3B + G)]`
- `A_U = 5 G_V/G_R + B_V/B_R − 6`: the universal anisotropy index, 0 for an isotropic
  crystal<Cite id="S-077" />
- `v_l = √[(B + 4G/3)/ρ]`, `v_t = √(G/ρ)` and `v_m = [(2/v_t³ + 1/v_l³)/3]^(−1/3)`: the longitudinal,
  transverse and average sound velocities (ρ is the density)
- `θ_D = (h/k_B) [3n N_A ρ / (4π M)]^(1/3) v_m`: the Debye temperature, with n the number of atoms per
  formula unit and M the molar mass<Cite id="S-078" />

</Section>

<Section id="indicators" heading="Reading the indicators">

| Indicator                     | Reading                                                                                                      |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Pugh ratio B/G                | Above 1.75: ductile. Below 1.75: brittle<Cite id="S-073" />                                                  |
| Poisson ratio ν               | Above 0.26: ductile. About 0.1: covalent, about 0.25: ionic, about 0.33: metallic bonding<Cite id="S-075" /> |
| Cauchy pressure `C₁₂ − C₄₄`   | Positive: metallic, ductile. Negative: directional (covalent) bonding<Cite id="S-079" />                     |
| Zener `A = 2C₄₄/(C₁₁ − C₁₂)`  | Anisotropy of a cubic crystal: 1 means isotropic                                                             |
| Vickers hardness (Chen model) | `H_V = 2(k²G)^0.585 − 3`, with `k = G/B` (in GPa); empirical<Cite id="S-074" />                              |

<Callout variant="note" title="Heuristic">

These readings are empirical rules of thumb for comparing materials, not measured properties. Use them
to describe trends, and say that they are estimates when you report them.

</Callout>

</Section>

<Section id="check" heading="Check before you move on">

- **The crystal passes the Born test:** all six stiffness eigenvalues are positive.
- **Cross-check the bulk modulus.** B_H from the Cij should agree with B0 from the
  [energy–volume fit](/workflow/equation-of-state/) within a few percent. A large disagreement points to
  an unconverged ENCUT or k-mesh, or to residual stress<Cite id="S-042" />.
- **The pre-relaxation was done at this ENCUT**, with the residual pressure within about ±1 kB.

</Section>

<Section id="energy-strain" heading="Alternative: the energy–strain method (VASPkit 201)">

VASPkit's authors also implement a route that fits energies instead of stresses, because energies are
less sensitive to numerical noise than stresses. It needs more runs: for a cubic crystal, 3 strain types
× 7 strains<Cite id="S-020" />. The steps:

1. Start from a fully relaxed, standardized conventional cell (603 or 604) and a fine k-mesh (102).
2. Use this INCAR for every strained cell. ISIF must be 2: the ions relax, while the cell is fixed by the
   applied strain.

<VaspFile
  name="INCAR"
  code={`PREC    = High
EDIFF   = 1E-8
ISIF    = 2        # must be 2: ions relax, cell is fixed by the strain
IBRION  = 2
NSW     = 100
EDIFFG  = -1E-2
LREAL   = F
ADDGRID = .TRUE.`}
/>

3. Write a `VPKIT.in` whose first line is `1` (pre-processing), then run 201. It creates one folder per
   strain, such as `C44/strain_-0.015` up to `strain_+0.015`.
4. Run VASP in every strain folder.
5. Change line 1 of `VPKIT.in` to `2` and run 201 again. It fits the energies and gives the same
   analysis as 203.

The `VPKIT.in` for a bulk crystal with 7 strains:

<VaspFile
  name="shell"
  code={`1                       ! 1 for pre-processing; 2 for post-processing
3D                      ! 2D for two-dimensional, 3D for bulk
7                       ! number of strain cases
-0.015 -0.010 -0.005 0.000 0.005 0.010 0.015   ! strain range`}
/>

</Section>
