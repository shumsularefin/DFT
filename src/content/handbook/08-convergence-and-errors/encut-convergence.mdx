<Why>

`ENCUT` is the first setting to test because it is cheap to scan and it affects everything after it. A cutoff that is too low gives wrong energies and, worse, wrong stress, so a cell can relax to the wrong size <Cite id="S-040" />. This topic walks through one complete test.

</Why>

<Intuition>

Picture raising the resolution of a photograph. The first steps make a big difference. After a while the picture looks the same however many pixels you add. You want the first resolution where doubling it would not change what you see, because every extra pixel costs time.

The scan is the same: raise `ENCUT` in even steps, run the same static calculation each time, and stop when the next step no longer changes the energy per atom by more than your threshold <Cite id="S-042" />.

</Intuition>

<TheMath>

The number of plane waves grows faster than the cutoff: it goes as the cutoff to the power 1.5, so doubling `ENCUT` gives almost three times as many plane waves (2 to the power 1.5 is about 2.8), and the run costs at least that much more <Cite id="S-049" />.

<Level min="intermediate">

The cost of the plane-wave part grows faster than linearly with the cutoff, because the number of plane waves in the basis grows as the cutoff to the power 3/2 <Cite id="S-049" />. This is why you stop at the first converged value instead of the highest you can afford.

</Level>

</TheMath>

<InVasp>

**Step 1. Find ENMAX.** Read it from your pseudopotential files with `grep ENMAX POTCAR`. This prints one number per element and does not show the file contents <Cite id="S-042" />.

<VaspFile name="shell" code={`grep ENMAX POTCAR`} />

**Step 2. Choose the range.** The author's rule: for runs that change the cell (`ISIF = 3`, elastic constants, equations of state) use `1.3 x max(ENMAX)`. For other runs use at least `max(ENMAX)` <Cite id="S-041" /> <Cite id="S-042" />. A scan from about `max(ENMAX)` up to a little above `1.3 x max(ENMAX)` therefore covers both. A common example is 400, 450, ..., 700 eV.

**Step 3. Write the runs.** In the [input builder](/tools/input-builder/), open the convergence series panel, give it the structure and the largest `ENMAX`, and download the folders as a zip. Each folder has the **convergence** preset INCAR with a different `ENCUT`. Keep the k-mesh the same in every folder.

**Step 4. Run and collect.** After the runs finish, take the energy per atom from each `OSZICAR` (the last `E0` value, divided by the atom count). Pair each with its `ENCUT`.

**Step 5. Plot.** Open the [convergence plotter](/tools/convergence/), paste the pairs, and read where the curve flattens. The plotter has a threshold slider (in meV per atom) and marks which runs have already converged for that threshold. Its pressure check uses a threshold of 5 kB that the tool labels a heuristic.

**Step 6. Choose and write it down.** Take that value, or the next one up if it is close. Use it for every run you will compare, and report it.

If the quantity you need is stress or pressure, test on the pressure as well. It converges later than the energy <Cite id="S-042" />.

</InVasp>

<WhatChanges>

| You choose...                     | You get...                                     | Cost             |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ---------------- |
| `ENCUT` below the converged value | Energies and stress that still drift           | Cheap, and wrong |
| The first converged `ENCUT`       | Energy changes below the threshold             | Reasonable       |
| A much higher `ENCUT`             | The same answer                                | Slow for no gain |
| `1.3 x max(ENMAX)` for `ISIF = 3` | Small Pulay stress, a cell that stops changing | Moderate         |

Why 1.3 times ENMAX for a relaxation: to keep the artificial Pulay stress small while the volume changes. What happens if you skip it: the cell relaxes to a size that depends on the starting volume <Cite id="S-040" />.

</WhatChanges>

<Mistakes>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Stopping at a single ENCUT because the default looks fine">

The default is the largest `ENMAX` in your `POTCAR` files <Cite id="S-019" />. It is often fine for a fixed-cell energy and often too low for a cell relaxation. Test it.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Mixing the scan with a different k-mesh">

If the mesh changes between folders you are measuring two things at once. Keep it fixed.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Judging convergence by the absolute energy">

Divide by the number of atoms, then compare successive steps. The absolute energy never reaches a nice round number.

</Callout>

</Mistakes>
