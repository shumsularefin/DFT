<Why>

A relaxation asks VASP to move the atoms until the forces are small. It fails in a few well-known ways: the optimizer loses its bearings (ZBRENT), it wanders around the minimum, the cell keeps drifting, or the vacuum in your slab disappears. Each has a short list of causes and a fix you can apply in a minute.

</Why>

<Intuition>

The optimizer is a hiker walking downhill in fog, using only the slope (the forces) under their feet. Two things make them lose their way: a slope reading that is noisy (loose electronic convergence), and steps that are too long near the bottom of the valley.

**Choosing the optimizer (IBRION).** 2 is conjugate gradient, robust when you start far from the minimum. 1 is a quasi-Newton method, efficient close to the minimum but able to be unstable when forces are large. 3 is damped molecular dynamics, useful for poor starting geometries <Cite id="S-019" />. A practical pattern is to start with 2 and switch to 1 for the final steps <Cite id="S-041" />.

**Always set IBRION yourself.** If you set NSW above 1 and leave IBRION out, it defaults to 0, which is molecular dynamics. OSZICAR then shows temperatures (`T=`) and the atoms fly around <Cite id="S-041" />.

**The usual problems.**

- **`ZBRENT: fatal error in bracketing`.** The line search could not bracket a minimum, usually because the forces are noisy or the structure is already near the minimum. Copy CONTCAR to POSCAR and restart, tighten EDIFF, switch to IBRION = 1 or lower POTIM. If the forces are already below your target, you may simply be done <Cite id="S-041" />.
- **It never finishes, or forces oscillate just above EDIFFG.** Tighten EDIFF (1E-6 or 1E-7) with NELMIN of 4 to 6, reduce POTIM to about 0.1, switch IBRION from 2 to 1 for the last steps, and for small cells use PREC = Accurate and LREAL = .FALSE. <Cite id="S-041" />.
- **The lattice keeps changing when you rerun ISIF = 3.** That is Pulay stress. Use ENCUT of about 1.3 times the largest ENMAX, rerun from CONTCAR until the volume stops changing (normally 2 to 3 rounds), and finish with a static run <Cite id="S-040" /> <Cite id="S-041" />.
- **The vacuum layer disappeared.** ISIF = 3 relaxes the cell, and the vacuum has no stress to resist compression. Use ISIF = 2 for slabs, surfaces and molecules in a box <Cite id="S-041" />.

<Level min="intermediate">

**Reaching NSW is not convergence.** Check that OUTCAR contains `reached required accuracy` before using the CONTCAR <Cite id="S-042" />. With a negative EDIFFG (a force criterion) the largest force must be below |EDIFFG|; the handbook calls force criteria the more reliable choice, and asks for EDIFF to be at least ten times smaller than any energy-based EDIFFG <Cite id="S-042" />.

**Restarting.** Copy CONTCAR to POSCAR and run again. Back up the OUTCAR of the first run if you want its history.

</Level>

<Level min="pro">

Noisy forces are the common root: forces are derivatives of the wavefunctions, so they need a tighter electronic convergence than the energy does, which is why the handbook's typical EDIFF is 1E-5 for relaxations, 1E-6 for static and DOS runs, and 1E-7 to 1E-8 for elastic constants and phonons <Cite id="S-042" />. ADDGRID is sometimes suggested for force noise; the handbook says to test whether it changes your forces rather than treat it as a general fix <Cite id="S-041" />.

</Level>

</Intuition>

<TheMath>

The stopping rule with a negative EDIFFG is $\max_I |\mathbf{F}_I| < |\text{EDIFFG}|$ over all atoms $I$. A positive EDIFFG instead stops when the energy change between ionic steps falls below it, which can stop a relaxation on a flat stretch that is not a minimum <Cite id="S-019" />.

</TheMath>

<InVasp>

A relaxation INCAR with the safeguards from this page <Cite id="S-041" />:

<VaspFile
  name="INCAR"
  code={`PREC = Accurate
ENCUT = 520
EDIFF = 1E-6
EDIFFG = -0.01
NSW = 100
IBRION = 2
ISIF = 3
POTIM = 0.1
ISMEAR = 0
SIGMA = 0.05
NELMIN = 4
LREAL = .FALSE.
LWAVE = .FALSE.
LCHARG = .FALSE.`}
/>

For a slab, change ISIF to 2. After a failure: `cp CONTCAR POSCAR`, then rerun. The [error finder](/errors/) has entries for ZBRENT, CNORMN and the relaxation-does-not-finish case.

</InVasp>

<WhatChanges>

| If you...              | ...then                                                              |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Lower POTIM            | Shorter steps: slower but more stable near the minimum               |
| Tighten EDIFF          | Less noisy forces; each ionic step costs a few more electronic steps |
| Switch IBRION 2 to 1   | Faster near the minimum; can be unstable when forces are large       |
| Use ISIF = 3 on a slab | The vacuum can collapse; use ISIF = 2                                |

Why these: each targets one of the failure modes above. What happens if you skip them: a relaxation that looks finished and is not, or a volume you cannot reproduce.

</WhatChanges>

<Mistakes>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Leaving IBRION out">

NSW above 1 with no IBRION runs molecular dynamics <Cite id="S-041" />.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Using the CONTCAR of a run that hit NSW">

It is the last structure reached, not a relaxed one. Look for `reached required accuracy` <Cite id="S-042" />.

</Callout>

</Mistakes>
