<Why>

A relaxation moves the atoms until the forces are small. It is a loop around the electronic loop, so it can fail in new ways: the search for the lowest energy may not close, the run may use up its steps, or the cell may drift to a size that is a result of the settings rather than the physics <Cite id="S-041" />.

</Why>

<Intuition>

A relaxation is a ball rolling into a valley. Three things go wrong. The ball can overshoot because the steps are too big (`POTIM`). It can wander on a nearly flat floor where forces are tiny and noisy, so it never quite stops. Or the map it follows is noisy because the electrons were not converged tightly enough, so the forces point slightly the wrong way <Cite id="S-041" />.

</Intuition>

<TheMath>

A relaxation moves the atoms downhill on the energy surface, one step at a time, and stops when the forces on them are small enough. Each method differs only in how it chooses the next step <Cite id="S-019" />.

<Level min="intermediate">

`IBRION = 2` uses a conjugate-gradient search with a line minimization along each direction; the `ZBRENT` error means that line search could not bracket a minimum <Cite id="S-041" />. `IBRION = 1` is a quasi-Newton scheme that is often better close to the minimum, and `IBRION = 3` is a damped-dynamics option the Field Handbook suggests for poor starting structures <Cite id="S-019" /> <Cite id="S-041" />. A negative `EDIFFG` sets the force criterion in eV per angstrom, and a positive one sets an energy criterion <Cite id="S-019" />.

</Level>

</TheMath>

<InVasp>

**ZBRENT: fatal error in bracketing.** Copy `CONTCAR` to `POSCAR` and restart. If it comes back, tighten `EDIFF`, switch to `IBRION = 1` or reduce `POTIM`. If the forces are already below your target, the structure may simply be converged <Cite id="S-041" />.

**The relaxation does not finish in `NSW` steps.** OSZICAR energies go up and down, and OUTCAR never prints "reached required accuracy". In order <Cite id="S-041" />:

1. Copy `CONTCAR` to `POSCAR` and continue.
2. Tighten `EDIFF` (1E-6 or 1E-7) and set `NELMIN` to 4 to 6.
3. Reduce `POTIM` to about 0.1.
4. Switch `IBRION = 2` to `1` for the final steps (or `3` for poor starts).
5. Use `PREC = Accurate` and `LREAL = .FALSE.` for small cells; test `ADDGRID`.

**I asked for a relaxation and OSZICAR shows temperatures.** `NSW > 1` without `IBRION` defaults to `IBRION = 0`, which is molecular dynamics. Set `IBRION = 2` (or 1) explicitly in every relaxation INCAR <Cite id="S-041" />.

**The cell keeps changing when I rerun `ISIF = 3`.** This is Pulay stress. Use `ENCUT` about `1.3 x max(ENMAX)`, rerun from `CONTCAR` until the volume stops changing (normally 2 to 3 rounds), then finish with a static run <Cite id="S-041" /> <Cite id="S-040" />.

**My slab's vacuum disappeared.** `ISIF = 3` relaxes the cell and vacuum has no stress to resist. Use `ISIF = 2` for slabs, surfaces and molecules in a box <Cite id="S-041" />.

**The ionic ladder.** When the relaxation itself is the problem, the Workflow Handbook gives this order <Cite id="S-042" />. First check that every ionic step converged electronically (see [the SCF topic](/handbook/08-convergence-and-errors/scf-trouble/)).

1. **Relax in stages.** `ISIF = 2` (ions only) first, then `ISIF = 3` from that `CONTCAR`. Why: a large starting stress together with bad positions is the usual cause of a runaway relaxation.
2. **Loose, then tight `EDIFFG`.** Start with an energy criterion (`EDIFFG = 1E-3`) or `-0.05` eV per angstrom, then tighten to `-0.005` and `-0.001`. Keep `EDIFF` at least 10 times smaller than an energy `EDIFFG`; with a force criterion use `EDIFF` of `1E-6` or smaller. Why: forces from a loosely converged density are noisy, and a tight criterion on noisy forces never closes.
3. **Step size.** Reduce `POTIM` (0.1 to 0.2) if the energy oscillates or `ZBRENT` complains. Why: smaller steps stop the overshoot.
4. **Switch optimizer.** `IBRION = 2` (conjugate gradient) far from the minimum, `1` (RMM-DIIS) close to it, `3` (damped MD, `POTIM` about 0.1) for very rough landscapes or soft modes. Why: conjugate gradient copes with large steps far from the minimum, RMM-DIIS is efficient close to it, and damped MD survives rough landscapes.
5. **Restart cleanly.** Copy `CONTCAR` to `POSCAR`; delete `WAVECAR` and `CHGCAR` if the cell changed a lot. Rerun until it converges in 1 to 3 steps. Why: this removes Pulay stress from the old basis.
6. **Check symmetry.** A symmetric start is a saddle point if the true minimum breaks the symmetry. Rattle the positions slightly (0.01 to 0.05 angstrom) or set `ISYM = 0`. Why: symmetry keeps the forces that would lead out of the saddle at zero.
7. **Fix what should not move.** Selective dynamics for the bottom layers of a slab; `ISIF = 2` for defects in a fixed host cell. Why: those atoms or that cell stand for the bulk and must not relax.

Before you use the result, check the four [signs of a good calculation](/handbook/08-convergence-and-errors/scf-trouble/) <Cite id="S-042" />.

For a printed message use the [error finder](/errors/).

</InVasp>

<WhatChanges>

| You change...   | ...the relaxation...                   | ...the risk                     |
| --------------- | -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| `POTIM` down    | Takes smaller steps                    | More steps                      |
| `EDIFF` tighter | Gets cleaner forces                    | Each electronic loop costs more |
| `IBRION` 2 to 1 | Often finishes near the minimum faster | Can fail from a poor start      |
| `ISIF` 3 to 2   | Keeps the cell fixed                   | No cell relaxation              |
| `ENCUT` up      | Smaller Pulay stress                   | More cost                       |

Why copy `CONTCAR` to `POSCAR`: the run continues from where it stopped, not from the beginning. What happens if you skip it: the same early steps are repeated.

</WhatChanges>

<Mistakes>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Leaving IBRION out">

With more than one ionic step and no `IBRION`, VASP runs molecular dynamics, not a relaxation <Cite id="S-041" />.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Using ISIF = 3 for a slab">

The vacuum collapses. Use `ISIF = 2` <Cite id="S-041" />.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Trusting a cell relaxation done once">

The first round measures the volume at one basis set. Rerun from `CONTCAR` until the volume stops changing, then finish with a static run <Cite id="S-041" />.

</Callout>

</Mistakes>
