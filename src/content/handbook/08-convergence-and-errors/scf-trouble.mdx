<Why>

The electronic loop (the <Term id="self-consistent-field">SCF</Term> loop) repeats until the energy stops changing. Usually it takes 10 to 20 steps. When it runs out of steps (`NELM`, 60 by default <Cite id="S-019" />) or the change keeps jumping about, the run is stuck. Most stuck runs are not caused by VASP settings. They are caused by the structure <Cite id="S-041" />.

</Why>

<Intuition>

Recall the loop: guess a density, build the potential, solve for the electrons, get a new density, mix it with the old one, repeat. It fails when the new density is very different from the old one every time, so the mix keeps overshooting. This is <Term id="charge-sloshing">charge sloshing</Term>: the charge rushes from one end of the cell to the other. It is common in metals, slabs and large cells, and in magnetic systems where the moments flip between two solutions <Cite id="S-041" />.

Read `OSZICAR` first. A healthy run shows the change in energy falling by orders of magnitude. A stuck run shows it jumping by about 1E-2 or more, or stalling, for all 60 lines of a step <Cite id="S-041" />.

</Intuition>

<TheMath>

Each SCF step mixes a fraction of the new density into the old one. A small fraction means small, safe steps that take longer; a large fraction means big steps that can overshoot and swing back and forth <Cite id="S-019" />.

<Level min="intermediate">

The mixing step builds the next input density from the previous output densities, with weights set by `AMIX` (the fraction of the new density that is mixed in, default 0.4 with PAW) and `BMIX` (a cutoff wave vector for the Kerker term, default 1.0) <Cite id="S-019" />. A smaller `AMIX` takes smaller steps and damps oscillation, at the price of more steps. For metals, values much smaller than the default are sometimes needed <Cite id="S-019" />.

</Level>

</TheMath>

<InVasp>

Work down this list in order. Stop as soon as the run converges <Cite id="S-041" />.

1. **Check the structure first.** Overlapping atoms or a broken geometry cause most "unconvergeable" runs. Look at it in a viewer.
2. **Change the algorithm and allow more steps.** Move through `ALGO` in one order, `Normal`, then `All`, then `Damped` (the reason is under rung 3 of the fuller ladder below), and set `NELM = 150` to 200.
3. **Reduce the mixing.** `AMIX = 0.1`, `BMIX = 0.0001` (linear mixing, for slabs and charge sloshing). For magnets also `AMIX_MAG = 0.4`, `BMIX_MAG = 0.0001`.
4. **Add a little smearing in metals** during the hard phase: `SIGMA` 0.1 to 0.2.
5. **Check the number of bands.** Raise `NBANDS` by about 20 percent when the highest bands are occupied.
6. **Start from something simpler.** Use a converged `WAVECAR` from an easier setting, such as PBE before a hybrid, or non-magnetic before magnetic.

This is a recovery INCAR for a hard case, not a starting point. Restore the normal settings for the production run if the result allows <Cite id="S-041" />.

**First decide which loop is failing.** In `OSZICAR`, lines starting with `DAV:` or `RMM:` are electronic steps and lines with `F=` are ionic steps. If every ionic step uses all `NELM` electronic steps, fix the electronic problem first: a relaxation cannot converge on forces from an unconverged density <Cite id="S-042" />.

Five commands from the handbook tell you which case you are in <Cite id="S-042" />:

<VaspFile
  name="shell"
  code={`tail -30 OSZICAR                               # dE oscillating or slowly decreasing?
grep -B1 "F=" OSZICAR | grep -E "DAV|RMM" | awk '{print \$2}'   # electronic steps used per ionic step
grep "reached required accuracy" OUTCAR        # ionic convergence reached?
grep -A \$((N+1)) "TOTAL-FORCE" OUTCAR | tail -\$N   # set N = number of atoms; forces on last step
grep "external pressure" OUTCAR | tail -3      # stress trend during ISIF=3`}
/>

The second line prints, for each ionic step, the number of its last electronic step. A column that keeps showing your `NELM` means the electronic loop is the one failing. Replace `N` in the fourth line with the number of atoms in your cell.

**The fuller ladder.** The Workflow Handbook gives a longer ladder for the electronic loop. Work down it and stop at the first rung that fixes the run <Cite id="S-042" />:

1. **Check the input.** Atoms too close, a `POSCAR` and `POTCAR` species order mismatch, a `MAGMOM` with the wrong count, an unreasonable starting moment. Why: no setting can rescue a wrong input.
2. **Tighten `EDIFF` in stages.** Run with `EDIFF = 1E-4` until stable, then restart from that result (`ISTART = 1`, `ICHARG = 1`) with `1E-5`, then `1E-6` (and `1E-8` for response calculations). Why: each stage starts closer to the solution, so the tight stage takes a few steps instead of wandering from random wavefunctions. The "Try Converge repeatedly" of the author's protocol (step 2 in [the workflow](/handbook/07-running/the-workflow/)) is this ladder.
3. **Change the algorithm.** `ALGO = Normal` (the most robust Davidson), then `All`, then `Damped` with `TIME = 0.4`. Avoid `Fast` and `VeryFast` for magnetic or +U systems. Why this order: the Workflow Handbook calls `Normal` (blocked Davidson) the most robust, so leaving `Fast` or `VeryFast` for `Normal` is the cheapest fix and comes first; `All` updates all bands together and `Damped` damps the update, and both handbooks name them for hard metals, magnets, +U and hybrids, so they come next. The Field Handbook's advice to use `All` or `Damped` is this second step, for a run where `Normal` has already stalled <Cite id="S-041" /> <Cite id="S-042" />.
4. **Damp the mixing.** `AMIX = 0.1`, `BMIX = 0.0001`, `AMIX_MAG = 0.4`, `BMIX_MAG = 0.0001` (linear mixing). For charge sloshing in metals and slabs reduce `AMIX` and `BMIX` gradually. Why: smaller steps stop the density from overshooting.
5. **More steps and a warm-up.** `NELM = 200` to 300, `NELMDL = -12`. Why: `NELMDL` sets the number of non-self-consistent steps at the start, so the wavefunctions settle before the density is updated <Cite id="S-019" />.
6. **Smearing.** A larger `SIGMA` (0.1 to 0.2) for a while, for metals or near-degenerate states, then reduce it and restart from `CHGCAR`. Why: smearing makes occupations change smoothly, so the loop stops flipping between states.
7. **Spin strategy.** Converge non-spin-polarized first, then restart spin-polarized from that `CHGCAR` (`ICHARG = 1`) with `MAGMOM`; or fix the moment with `NUPDOWN`, converge, then release it. Why: the moments are the hardest part; give them a converged density to start from.
8. **DFT+U and hybrids.** Converge plain PBE first and restart from its `WAVECAR`; ramp `U` in steps; set `LMAXMIX = 4` (d) or `6` (f). If a hybrid (HSE) run still diverges, use `ALGO = All` or `Damped` with a smaller `TIME` (0.2). Why: these functionals converge badly from a random start.
9. **Numerics.** Change `NCORE`, `KPAR` or the core count; `LREAL = .FALSE.`; `PREC = Accurate`. Why: small odd behaviour can come from parallel round-off.

**Signs of a good calculation** <Cite id="S-042" />. Check all four before you use the result:

- Every ionic step converged electronically in fewer than `NELM` steps.
- `OUTCAR` prints "reached required accuracy", the final forces are below `|EDIFFG|`, and the pressure is within about plus or minus 1 to 2 kB.
- The moments and the symmetry are the ones you intended, or you understand why they changed.
- The smearing entropy (`EENTRO`) is below about 1 meV per atom.

For a quick answer to a printed message, use the [error finder](/errors/).

</InVasp>

<WhatChanges>

| You change...       | ...the loop...                            | ...the risk                             |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| `ALGO` to `All`     | Is more robust                            | Slower per step                         |
| `NELM` up           | Gets more steps to finish                 | Hides a loop that will never converge   |
| `AMIX` down         | Takes smaller, calmer steps               | More steps are needed                   |
| `SIGMA` up (metals) | Smooths the Fermi surface                 | Moves the energy; reduce it again later |
| `NBANDS` up         | Gives empty bands somewhere to put charge | Slightly slower                         |

Why this order: the cheapest and most likely cause comes first. What happens if you skip step 1: you can spend days tuning mixing for a structure that was wrong.

</WhatChanges>

<Mistakes>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Raising NELM to 500 and hoping">

A loop that will not converge in 200 steps seldom converges in 500. Change something else <Cite id="S-041" />.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Accepting the result of an unconverged SCF">

VASP still writes output when `NELM` is reached. Check that `OSZICAR` shows the last change below `EDIFF`, not just that the file exists.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Leaving the recovery settings in the production INCAR">

A larger `SIGMA` or heavy damping can bias the result. Use them to get a first converged `WAVECAR`, then restart with the normal settings.

</Callout>

</Mistakes>
