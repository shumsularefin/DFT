<Why>

The k-mesh is the second setting you must test on every new system. A mesh that is too coarse gives wrong energies, a wrong density of states and, for metals, forces that do not settle <Cite id="S-042" />. A mesh that is finer than needed multiplies the cost for no benefit.

</Why>

<Intuition>

A crystal has an endless number of electron states, grouped by their <Term id="k-point">k-point</Term>. VASP cannot add them all, so it adds a finite mesh and treats each point as a stand-in for its neighbours. That is fine when the electrons change slowly from point to point, as in an insulator. In a metal the occupation jumps sharply at the Fermi surface, so a coarse mesh either catches the jump or misses it, and the energy depends on which. That is why metals need a finer mesh and more careful testing <Cite id="S-041" /> <Cite id="S-050" />.

<Term id="smearing">Smearing</Term> softens the jump so the sum settles faster, but it is a second
approximation. It does not rescue a mesh that is too coarse, so test the mesh and the smearing as a pair
<Cite id="S-041" />.

</Intuition>

<TheMath>

A k-point mesh averages over the crystal by sampling a finite set of points, and more points give a better average. In a metal the quantity being averaged has a sharp edge at the Fermi level, which is why metals need denser meshes and a little smearing <Cite id="S-042" />.

<Level min="intermediate">

For a metal the total energy depends on the smearing width `SIGMA` and the mesh together: the energy error from the mesh falls as the mesh is refined, while the error from smearing grows with `SIGMA` <Cite id="S-015" />. A converged calculation keeps both small. The entropy term `T*S` that VASP prints (`EENTRO` in `OUTCAR`) measures the effect of smearing, and the author's check is that `T*S` divided by the number of atoms stays below about 1 meV <Cite id="S-042" />.

</Level>

</TheMath>

<InVasp>

**The scan.** Run static calculations on a rising series of Gamma-centred meshes, for example 4x4x4, then 6x6x6, then 8x8x8, with `ENCUT` fixed, until the energy per atom changes by less than about 1 meV <Cite id="S-041" />.

**Resolution.** VASPKIT and the builder describe a mesh by its resolution in 2 pi per angstrom. The author lists 0.03 to 0.04 as usually enough, finer (0.02 to 0.01) for metals or accurate densities of states, and Gamma-only for large cells <Cite id="S-042" />. These are rules of thumb: the test decides.

**Metals versus insulators.**

| System                   | Smearing the author uses              | Mesh                            |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Insulator, semiconductor | `ISMEAR = 0`, `SIGMA` 0.01 to 0.05 eV | Coarser usually passes the test |
| Metal                    | `ISMEAR = 1`, `SIGMA` 0.1 to 0.2 eV   | Finer; test more carefully      |

`ISMEAR = 0` with a small `SIGMA` such as 0.05 is the safe choice for anything <Cite id="S-041" /> <Cite id="S-042" />. Never use `ISMEAR = 1` for a semiconductor <Cite id="S-042" />.

**Smearing test.** After the mesh looks converged, check the entropy term:

<VaspFile name="shell" code={`grep EENTRO OUTCAR | tail -1`} />

Divide by the number of atoms. If it is above about 1 meV, reduce `SIGMA` and test the mesh again <Cite id="S-042" />.

**Tetrahedron method.** `ISMEAR = -5` needs at least four irreducible k-points; a large cell or a Gamma-only run has fewer, and VASP stops with "Tetrahedron method fails". Use `ISMEAR = 0` there <Cite id="S-041" />.

</InVasp>

<WhatChanges>

| If you...                        | ...then...                                          | Cost                      |
| -------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Use a coarser mesh               | Energies and DOS shift, metals most of all          | Cheaper, risky            |
| Refine the mesh                  | The energy settles; it may wiggle on the way        | Grows with the mesh size  |
| Raise `SIGMA`                    | Energy converges faster but moves from the true one | Cheaper, biased           |
| Use `ISMEAR = -5` on a tiny mesh | VASP stops with an error                            | None, but the run is lost |

Why test on a mesh series: the right mesh depends on the cell size and on whether the material is a metal. What happens if you skip it: a result that depends on an arbitrary choice.

</WhatChanges>

<Mistakes>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Treating one smooth step as proof">

For a metal the energy can move up and down as the mesh changes. Check two or three steps in a row before you stop.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Using the same mesh for a cell and its supercell">

A larger real-space cell has a smaller Brillouin zone, so it needs a coarser mesh. Compare mesh density (resolution), not the numbers 4x4x4 <Cite id="S-050" />.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Hiding a poor mesh behind a large SIGMA">

Smearing makes the energy look settled while moving it from the answer you wanted. Keep `SIGMA` small and keep the entropy term below the threshold.

</Callout>

</Mistakes>
