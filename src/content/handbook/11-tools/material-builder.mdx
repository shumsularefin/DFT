<Why>

Most VASP problems that look mysterious start in the `POSCAR`: two atoms on top of each other, a cell vector typed wrong, a species line in a different order from the `POTCAR`. The <a href="/builder/">Material Builder</a> lets you make and edit a structure in the browser and checks it while you work, so these mistakes show up before a job is queued. This page builds the cubic $\mathrm{LaCuO}_3$ cell of module 10 with it, makes the supercell an oxygen-vacancy calculation needs, and explains the arithmetic behind supercells and slabs so you can check what any tool gives you.

</Why>

<Intuition>

**What the Builder is.** In crystal mode it shows the cell in 3D, a table of sites with fractional coordinates, a toolbar (template, undo and redo, wrap, merge duplicates, gentle relax, a fixed 2×2×2 supercell, a defect supercell, import of a POSCAR or CIF) and a validity panel with the checks B001 to B012. It exports POSCAR or CIF and hands the structure to the Input Builder, the k-path tool or the magnetic-orderings tool. A molecule mode draws molecules on a 2D canvas, makes a rough 3D geometry ("Clean up in 3D", marked heuristic) and puts the molecule in a vacuum box.

**Walkthrough: cubic LaCuO3.**

1. Choose the template **Cubic perovskite (SrTiO3)**. You get five sites: Sr at the corner, Ti at the centre, three O on the face centres, in a cubic cell of 3.905 Å.
2. Click **Element** and pick La from the periodic table, then press **Sub La** on the Sr row. Pick Cu and press **Sub Cu** on the Ti row.
3. Read the validity panel. For this cell it shows four messages: B005 (charge neutral only with a rare oxidation state, since neutrality needs $\mathrm{Cu}^{3+}$), B006 (the cation is small for its coordination, Pauling's first rule), B008 (the perovskite check with $t$ and $\mu$: $t$ near 1, $\mu$ below the usual limit, as on the first page of module 10) and B011 (symmetry not analysed, an info line) <Cite id="S-002" /> <Cite id="S-022" />. None is an error, so export is allowed; the warnings are the same concerns module 10 worked through.
4. The Builder has no field for the lattice parameter. To use the cell of the structure page, import its POSCAR with **Import POSCAR/CIF**, or export this one and edit the scale line.
5. Press **Export POSCAR**, or **Magnetic orderings** to send the cell to the tool that writes the G-type cell and `MAGMOM`.

**Walkthrough: an oxygen vacancy.** Fully oxidized $\mathrm{LaCuO}_3$ is hard to make and oxygen-deficient phases are common <Cite id="S-096" />, so a vacancy calculation is a natural next step. Press **Defect supercell (>= 10 A)**: the Builder searches supercells up to 64 copies for the one with the fewest atoms whose shortest lattice vector is at least 10 Å. For the template cell it picks 16 copies, 80 atoms, with a shortest vector of about 11 Å, rather than the 3×3×3 cell with 135 atoms. Then press **Vacancy** on one O row: the cell becomes $\mathrm{La_{16}Cu_{16}O_{47}}$. The 10 Å is a convention, not a converged value, so test the property you want against a larger cell.

</Intuition>

<TheMath>

**Supercells.** Write the lattice vectors as the rows of a matrix $L$. A supercell is $L' = M L$ with an integer matrix $M$; it holds $\det M$ copies of the original cell, so $N' = \det M \cdot N$ atoms. The Builder's 2×2×2 button uses $M = 2I$, $\det M = 8$, 40 atoms for the perovskite. The defect supercell above is

$$M = \begin{pmatrix} 4 & 0 & 0 \\ 2 & 2 & 0 \\ 2 & 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \det M = 16,$$

whose vectors $4\mathbf a_1$, $2\mathbf a_1 + 2\mathbf a_2$ and $2\mathbf a_1 + 2\mathbf a_3$ are all at least $2\sqrt 2\, a$ long. The search runs over matrices in Hermite normal form (lower triangular, with each off-diagonal entry smaller than the diagonal one in its column), which list every distinct supercell of a given size exactly once.

**Validity: the overlap test.** For every pair of atoms $i, j$, including periodic images, the Builder compares the shortest distance $d_{ij}$ with the sum of covalent radii $r_i + r_j$ from Cordero et al. <Cite id="S-021" /> (as transcribed in ASE <Cite id="S-052" />):

$$d_{ij} < 0.5\,(r_i + r_j) \Rightarrow \text{B001 error (overlap)}, \qquad d_{ij} < 0.75\,(r_i + r_j) \Rightarrow \text{B002 warning}.$$

An error blocks export unless you type a reason, which is then written into the file's comment line.

<Level min="intermediate">

**Slabs.** The site has slab-building code that follows Sun and Ceder <Cite id="S-064" />, but the Builder has no slab button yet, so here is what such a tool does. For planes $(hkl)$ the spacing is $d_{hkl} = 1/\lvert\mathbf G_{hkl}\rvert$ with $\mathbf G_{hkl} = h\mathbf b_1 + k\mathbf b_2 + l\mathbf b_3$ (reciprocal vectors without the $2\pi$); for a cubic cell $d_{hkl} = a/\sqrt{h^2+k^2+l^2}$. A minimum thickness $t_\mathrm{min}$ is rounded up to whole repeats,

$$n = \left\lceil \frac{t_\mathrm{min}}{d_{hkl}} \right\rceil, \qquad c_\mathrm{slab} = n\, d_{hkl} + t_\mathrm{vac},$$

with defaults of 10 Å for $t_\mathrm{min}$ and 15 Å of vacuum in the site's code. For the perovskite (001) planes, $d_{001} = a$, so about three cells give 10 Å.

</Level>

<Level min="pro">

**Polarity.** In the ionic picture a (001) slab of $\mathrm{LaCuO}_3$ alternates $\mathrm{LaO}^+$ and $\mathrm{CuO_2}^-$ layers. Stacked charged layers make a dipole that grows with thickness, Tasker's type 3 surface <Cite id="S-063" />, and the energy then converges badly. A symmetric slab with the same termination on both sides, or a dipole correction, is needed. Relax any slab or molecule in vacuum with `ISIF = 2`, so that the vacuum is not squeezed out <Cite id="S-041" />.

</Level>

</TheMath>

`ISIF = 2` keeps the bulk lattice, which is the usual choice for a dilute defect. Check the species counts in the exported POSCAR before you write `MAGMOM`; a removed atom changes the count of its species only.

</InVasp>

<WhatChanges>

| Builder action             | What it does to the structure                                     | What to check                                      |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Template, then Sub         | Changes the species on a site                                     | Species line order and the POTCAR order            |
| Gentle relax               | Pushes apart atoms closer than 0.75 of the radius sum, cell fixed | Not a physical relaxation; VASP still relaxes      |
| 2x2x2 supercell            | $\det M = 8$ copies                                               | Atom count and k-mesh for the larger cell          |
| Defect supercell (>= 10 A) | Fewest atoms with images at least 10 Å apart                      | Convergence of the defect property with size       |
| Vacancy                    | Removes one site                                                  | The `MAGMOM` count and the charge state you intend |
| Export with an error       | Needs a written reason                                            | The reason in the comment line                     |

Why build here: the checks catch overlaps and impossible cells before VASP does. Why know the formulas: any supercell or slab tool can be checked by hand with $\det M$ and $d_{hkl}$. What happens if you skip the checks: VASP may run with overlapping atoms and fail, or worse, converge to nonsense.

</WhatChanges>

<Mistakes>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Using the 2x2x2 cell for a defect without checking the image distance">

For a 3.9 Å perovskite the 2×2×2 cell puts a defect only about 7.8 Å from its image. Fix: use the defect supercell, read its shortest vector, and test a larger cell for the property you need.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Taking Gentle relax for a relaxation">

Gentle relax only pushes atoms apart by a geometric rule, at most 0.05 Å per step, with the cell fixed. Fix: use it to clear overlaps, then relax in VASP.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Ignoring the warnings because export is allowed">

For $\mathrm{LaCuO}_3$ the B005, B006 and B008 warnings are real chemistry: a rare oxidation state and a small B cation. Fix: read each warning and decide whether it matters, as module 10 did.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Relaxing a slab or molecule with ISIF = 3">

The stress of the empty vacuum pushes the cell to shrink. Fix: use `ISIF = 2` for anything with vacuum <Cite id="S-041" />.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Forgetting MAGMOM after removing an atom">

The atom count drops by one, and a `MAGMOM` with the old count no longer matches. Fix: recount from the species line of the exported POSCAR.

</Callout>

</Mistakes>
