<Why>

Finishing the handbook gives you the core mental models and browser tools to run trustworthy VASP workflows; the next step is scaling up to automated scripts and literature-grade verification <Cite id="S-042" />.

</Why>

<Intuition>

Once you are comfortable running single calculations and checking `OUTCAR` by hand, Python libraries such as `pymatgen`, `ASE`, and `phonopy` let you automate high-throughput workflows, phase diagrams, and full phonon dispersions across the entire Brillouin zone <Cite id="S-024" />. Keep returning to the <a href="/tools/review/">Calculation Review</a> and <a href="/errors/">Error Finder</a> whenever an automated job behaves unexpectedly.

<Scene id="fcc" />

For every calculation you plan to publish, verify your primary sources on <a href="/references/">References</a> and record every numerical parameter and convergence check in your supporting information <Cite id="S-019" />.

</Intuition>

<TheMath>

Reproducibility in computational materials science rests on three pillars: exact input provenance (`POSCAR`, `INCAR`, `KPOINTS`, `POTCAR` titles), explicit convergence criteria, and honest reporting of functional limitations <Cite id="S-042" />.

</TheMath>

<Mistakes>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Running high-throughput scripts without spot-checking individual OUTCARs">

Automated pipelines can silently accept unconverged electronic steps or collapsed magnetic moments if exit codes are the only check. Always audit representative runs manually.

</Callout>

</Mistakes>
