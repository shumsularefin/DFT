<Why>

Before a project like the $\mathrm{RCuO}_3$ example of module 10 there is a quieter step: deciding which compound is worth the compute at all. Materials databases hold hundreds of thousands of computed entries, and a few filters (elements, stability, cell size) can reduce a family to a shortlist in minutes <Cite id="S-024" />. The Screening Studio does that filtering in the browser and turns one candidate into a first-project plan: what makes it hard, roughly what it costs, and three starter INCARs. This page walks through it once with a real question and explains the two numbers it relies on, the energy above the hull and the relative cost.

</Why>

<Intuition>

**Read the banner first.** Until the site's author runs a real data pull from the Materials Project, the <a href="/studio/">Screening Studio</a> loads a small synthetic table: 30 entries whose formulas, volumes, densities and space groups come from the site's test structures, while every energy, band gap and magnetic value is made up. The banner at the top says so, and so does this page. Everything below describes how the tool works; none of the numbers it shows today is a property of a real material.

**A walkthrough: copper oxides for a first project.**

1. Open the Studio and read the banner and the line under the filters ("Showing ... of ... matching material(s)").
2. In **Must contain elements** type `Cu, O`. Only entries that contain both elements remain. In the demo table that leaves one row, `NdCuO3`, a 20-site orthorhombic cell.
3. Try the presets to see what they set: **Beginner-friendly** (at most 10 sites and on the hull), **Stable oxides** (contains O and on the hull) and **Magnetic candidates** (magnetic only). With `Cu, O` typed, the Beginner-friendly preset clears the element field, so set filters after a preset, not before.
4. Narrow further with **Max E_hull (eV/atom)**, **Max sites**, **Crystal system**, the **On hull (stable)** and **Magnetic only** boxes, and sort by energy above hull, band gap, sites, density or formula.
5. Press **Export selection CSV** to keep the shortlist, then **Inspect** on a row. The details panel offers the **Feasibility & First-Project Card**, a link to the tolerance-factor tool for perovskite-like formulas, and the Input Builder.

**The Project Card.** For the `NdCuO3` row the card lists six rules, each with a verdict and a reason <Cite id="S-042" />:

| Rule       | What it looks at                         | For this row                       |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| `F-SIZE`   | Atoms in the cell: 10 or fewer is green  | Yellow: 20 atoms, 11 to 30         |
| `F-FBLOCK` | Lanthanide or actinide f elements        | Yellow: contains Nd                |
| `F-MAG`    | Magnetic flag or ordering in the dataset | Yellow: spin-polarized             |
| `F-METAL`  | Metallic flag in the dataset             | Yellow: denser k-mesh needed       |
| `F-HEAVY`  | Elements with atomic number above 54     | Yellow: Nd, spin-orbit may matter  |
| `F-PAW`    | A recommended PAW potential per element  | Green when every element is listed |

The overall verdict is the worst single verdict. The magnetic and metallic flags here are synthetic, so on this row the card shows how the rules work, not what $\mathrm{NdCuO}_3$ is like. On a real snapshot the same row would tell you what module 10 found the hard way: an f element, a $d$ shell that needs care, and a cell four times larger than the cubic one.

</Intuition>

<TheMath>

**Energy above the hull.** Take all computed phases in a chemical system and plot their formation energy per atom against composition. The lower convex envelope of those points is the hull. A compound with energy per atom $E(c)$ at composition $c$ sits a distance

$$E_\mathrm{above\ hull}(c) = E(c) - E_\mathrm{hull}(c) \ge 0$$

above it, where $E_\mathrm{hull}(c)$ is the hull energy at that composition <Cite id="S-102" />. In a binary system, between two hull phases $\alpha$ and $\beta$ at compositions $x_\alpha < x < x_\beta$, the hull is the straight line

$$E_\mathrm{hull}(x) = E_\alpha + \frac{x - x_\alpha}{x_\beta - x_\alpha}\,(E_\beta - E_\alpha).$$

A compound on the hull has $E_\mathrm{above\ hull} = 0$: no mixture of other phases in the database is lower in energy. A compound above it would, at zero temperature and in that database, lower its energy by decomposing into the hull phases at its composition. The Studio does not build the hull itself; it shows the `energy_above_hull` field of the dataset <Cite id="S-044" />.

**Relative cost.** The Project Card gives no hours. It gives a relative cost against a four-atom non-magnetic cell at the same settings:

$$\text{relative cost} \approx \left(\frac{N_\mathrm{sites}}{4}\right)^3 \times \begin{cases} 2 & \text{spin-polarized} \\ 1 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

The cube is the large-cell scaling of the orthogonalization steps of plane-wave codes <Cite id="S-010" />, and spin polarization solves two spin channels <Cite id="S-019" />. For the 20-site magnetic row: $(20/4)^3 \times 2 = 250$. The card says the same, with the warning that small cells cost relatively more than this formula suggests.

<Level min="intermediate">

**What the hull does not say.** The hull depends on which phases the database contains and on the functional; a phase missing from the database cannot appear on the hull. Small positive values are often still synthesizable, because entropy, kinetics or pressure can stabilize them, and $\mathrm{LaCuO}_3$ itself needs high oxygen pressure to form. Treat a maximum `E_hull` as a soft filter and look at the entries just above your threshold.

</Level>

<Level min="pro">

The `ml_e_above_hull` column of the data schema is reserved and empty, and the Studio explains under "Why there is no ML column yet" that no machine-learned estimate is shown. When a real snapshot is loaded, its manifest records the database version and query, which belong in your methods section next to the date of the pull <Cite id="S-044" />.

</Level>

</TheMath>

<InVasp>

**From the card to VASP.** The card writes three starter steps (relaxation, static SCF with DOS, band structure on a path) from the author's protocol <Cite id="S-042" />, using the `ENCUT` you enter (set it to at least 1.3 times the largest `ENMAX` of your POTCARs). For a magnetic metal such as the demo `NdCuO3` row, step 1 reads:

<VaspFile
  name="INCAR"
  code={`SYSTEM = NdCuO3 relax
ENCUT = 520
PREC = Accurate
EDIFF = 1E-6
EDIFFG = -0.02
IBRION = 2
ISIF = 3
NSW = 60
ISMEAR = 1
SIGMA = 0.2
KSPACING = 0.25
ISPIN = 2
LORBIT = 11`}
  annotations={{
    2: 'the value you type on the card',
    9: 'Methfessel-Paxton, because the row is flagged metallic',
    12: 'because the row is flagged magnetic',
  }}
/>

This is a skeleton, not a finished input. For a compound like this you still add what module 10 needed: a `MAGMOM` line with one entry per atom, `LMAXMIX = 4` and `LASPH` for the $d$ and $f$ shells, a decision about $U$ and about the Nd $4f$ electrons, the spin starts, and your own convergence tests. The **Customize in Input Builder** button and the k-path link on the card take you to the tools that do that. With `ISMEAR = 1`, check that the smearing entropy per atom stays small, and use `ISMEAR = 0` for the final DOS <Cite id="S-042" />.

**Before you trust a row.** Open the entry on the Materials Project itself (from a real snapshot), check its calculation type and magnetic ordering, and remember that its band gap comes from a semilocal functional <Cite id="S-024" />.

</InVasp>

<WhatChanges>

| Filter or control      | What it narrows                     | What to keep in mind                             |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Must contain elements  | Chemistry                           | Every listed element must be present             |
| Max E_hull             | Thermodynamic stability             | Soft filter; metastable phases can still be made |
| Max sites              | Cost                                | Cost grows roughly as the cube of the atom count |
| Crystal system         | Symmetry                            | A tilted perovskite is not cubic                 |
| On hull, Magnetic only | Stable entries, spin-polarized ones | Flags come from the dataset, not from the Studio |
| Project Card `ENCUT`   | The starter INCARs                  | Set from your POTCAR `ENMAX`, then converge      |

Why screen: a few filters prevent weeks spent on an unstable or very large cell. Why look at the card: it names the hard parts before you meet them in `OUTCAR`. What happens if you skip both: the first sign of trouble is a failed or unaffordable run.

</WhatChanges>

<Mistakes>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Quoting numbers from the demo table">

The current table is synthetic: its energies, gaps and moments are made up. Fix: read the banner, and quote database values only from a real snapshot, with its version.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Treating a database band gap as a measured gap">

Database gaps come from semilocal (or $+U$) functionals, which usually underestimate gaps <Cite id="S-024" />. Fix: use them to sort candidates, not as predictions.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Reading E_hull = 0 as 'can be made'">

On the hull means lowest in that database at zero temperature; it says nothing about kinetics or synthesis conditions <Cite id="S-102" />. Fix: check the literature, and look at entries just above the hull too.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Running the starter INCAR as it is">

The starter steps have no `MAGMOM`, no `LMAXMIX` and no $U$. Fix: use them as a skeleton and complete them in the Input Builder, as in module 10.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Taking the relative cost as wall time">

The estimate is a ratio to a small cell at the same settings, not hours. Fix: time one short run of your own and scale from that.

</Callout>

</Mistakes>
