<Why>

Almost every project starts with a relaxation: move the atoms (and perhaps the cell) until the forces vanish, then compute everything else on that structure. A wrong geometry spoils every later number, from the band gap to the formation energy.

The trap is that a relaxation can finish without an error and still be unfinished. It may have stopped because it ran out of steps (`NSW`), because a loose energy criterion was met while the forces were still large, or because the cell grew and the fixed plane-wave basis no longer describes it well <Cite id="S-041" />. Judging a relaxation means checking each of these.

</Why>

<Intuition>

A relaxation is a ball rolling downhill on the energy landscape. Three choices shape the walk.

**How the ball moves (`IBRION`).** VASP offers three optimizers <Cite id="S-041" />:

- `IBRION = 2`, **conjugate gradient**: robust, the standard for a rough starting structure. It searches along a direction until the energy stops falling, then picks a new direction.
- `IBRION = 1`, **RMM-DIIS quasi-Newton**: fast close to the minimum, because it learns the shape of the valley from previous steps. Far from the minimum, where the forces are large, it can be unstable.
- `IBRION = 3`, **damped molecular dynamics**: the atoms move as if under friction. Slow but forgiving, for poor starting geometries and soft landscapes.

A common pattern for a hard case is conjugate gradient for the first steps, then RMM-DIIS to finish <Cite id="S-041" />.

**What may move (`ISIF`).** Positions only, or also the cell shape and the volume. A slab or a defect in a fixed host cell moves ions only; a bulk crystal whose lattice constant you want relaxes all three <Cite id="S-041" />. Individual atoms can also be frozen with <Term id="selective-dynamics">selective dynamics</Term> in `POSCAR`.

**When to stop (`EDIFFG`).** A negative value is a force criterion: stop when every force is below its absolute value, in eV per angstrom. A positive value stops when the energy change between ionic steps is small, which can happen while forces are still large. The default is positive (`EDIFF` times 10), so set a negative value on purpose <Cite id="S-041" />.

**Before you trust `CONTCAR`**, check four things <Cite id="S-042" />:

1. `OUTCAR` contains `reached required accuracy`.
2. Every ionic step converged electronically in fewer than `NELM` steps.
3. The forces fell steadily rather than oscillating.
4. If the cell changed, a second run from `CONTCAR` changes it no further (see the Pulay stress below).

</Intuition>

<TheMath>

The quantity a force criterion tests is the largest force magnitude over all $N$ atoms:

$$F_{\max} = \max_{1 \le i \le N} \lVert\mathbf F_i\rVert$$

With `EDIFFG` negative, the relaxation stops when $F_{\max}$ falls below the absolute value of `EDIFFG`. With `EDIFFG` positive, it stops when $\lvert E_{n} - E_{n-1}\rvert$ between successive ionic steps falls below `EDIFFG` in eV <Cite id="S-019" /> <Cite id="S-041" />.

<Level min="intermediate">

**What each optimizer does with `POTIM`** <Cite id="S-041" /> <Cite id="S-019" />:

- **Quasi-Newton** (`IBRION = 1`) steps $\Delta\mathbf R = \mathbf H^{-1}\mathbf F$, where $\mathbf H^{-1}$ is an estimate of the inverse curvature built from the history of forces and positions (`NFREE` sets how many past steps are kept). `POTIM` scales the steps. It needs accurate forces, so pair it with a tight `EDIFF`.
- **Conjugate gradient** (`IBRION = 2`) makes a trial step along a search direction, scaled by `POTIM`, then a line minimization along it; each new direction is chosen conjugate to the previous ones. A `ZBRENT` error means the line search could not bracket a minimum, usually because forces or energies were noisy.
- **Damped dynamics** (`IBRION = 3`) integrates a damped equation of motion of the form $m\ddot{\mathbf R} = \mathbf F - \gamma\dot{\mathbf R}$: `POTIM` acts as the time step and `SMASS` sets the damping. Too large a `POTIM` and the atoms fly past the minimum.

For all three the default `POTIM` is 0.5; reduce it (to about 0.1) when a relaxation oscillates. For molecular dynamics (`IBRION = 0`) `POTIM` is the time step in fs and has no default <Cite id="S-041" />.

</Level>

<Level min="pro">

**Why volume relaxations need care.** During a run VASP keeps the set of plane waves chosen for the starting cell. When the cell expands, the same plane waves correspond to a lower effective cutoff, and the incomplete basis adds a spurious, nearly isotropic term to the diagonal of the stress, the <Term id="pulay-stress">Pulay stress</Term> <Cite id="S-040" />. The stress is the strain derivative of the energy <Cite id="S-092" /> and converges with the cutoff much more slowly than the energy itself, so cell relaxations use `ENCUT` of about 1.3 times the largest `ENMAX` (the VASP wiki notes the default often has to be raised by about 30 percent for the stress) <Cite id="S-042" />. Restarting from `CONTCAR` builds a new basis for the new cell, so repeated runs converge to the right volume, normally within 2 to 3 rounds <Cite id="S-041" />. An energy-volume curve from fixed-volume runs avoids the problem altogether; see [the equation of state topic](/handbook/12-properties/equation-of-state/).

</Level>

</TheMath>

<TagVisual tag="ISIF" />

**What each `ISIF` moves**, from the Field Handbook table <Cite id="S-041" />:

| `ISIF` | Stress computed | Ions | Cell shape | Cell volume | Typical use                                     |
| ------ | --------------- | ---- | ---------- | ----------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| 0      | no              | yes  | no         | no          | MD without stress (fastest)                     |
| 1      | trace only      | yes  | no         | no          | Rarely used                                     |
| 2      | yes             | yes  | no         | no          | Fixed-cell relaxation: defects, surfaces, slabs |
| 3      | yes             | yes  | yes        | yes         | Full relaxation of a bulk crystal               |
| 4      | yes             | yes  | yes        | no          | Relax at fixed volume, energy-volume curves     |
| 5      | yes             | no   | yes        | no          | Cell shape only                                 |
| 6      | yes             | no   | yes        | yes         | Cell shape and volume, ions fixed               |
| 7      | yes             | no   | no         | yes         | Isotropic volume change only                    |
| 8      | yes             | yes  | no         | yes         | Ions and volume (VASP 6)                        |

The default is `ISIF = 2` for relaxations and `0` for MD. Nothing moves unless `IBRION` is above 0 and `NSW` is above 0. `ISIF = 3` keeps the starting space group unless the input breaks it <Cite id="S-041" />.

<TagVisual tag="EDIFFG" />

- **`EDIFFG` by purpose** <Cite id="S-041" />: about -0.03 to -0.05 for screening, -0.01 to -0.02 for publication structures, -0.001 to -0.005 before phonons and finite differences. A tight `EDIFFG` needs a tight `EDIFF`, or the forces stay noisy and the ions chatter around the minimum.
- **Selective dynamics.** Add a line starting with `S` after the atom counts, then three flags per atom: `T` may move, `F` is frozen. The usual use is the bottom layers of a slab, which stand for the bulk. `CONTCAR` keeps the flags <Cite id="S-041" />.

- **Restarting.** If the run stopped at `NSW` or ran out of time, look at `CONTCAR` (a killed job can leave it empty), then `cp CONTCAR POSCAR` and run again <Cite id="S-041" />.
- **After `ISIF = 3`**, repeat from `CONTCAR` until the volume stops changing, then do a static run (`NSW = 0`, `IBRION = -1`) on the final structure for the energy <Cite id="S-041" /> <Cite id="S-042" />.
- **Read the stress separately.** The force criterion ignores the stress; in `ISIF = 3` runs check the `in kB` line of `OUTCAR` as well <Cite id="S-041" />.
- **When it will not converge**, work through [the relaxation trouble topic](/handbook/08-convergence-and-errors/relaxation-trouble/): staged relaxation, smaller `POTIM`, a change of optimizer.

</InVasp>

<WhatChanges>

| Observation in the output                  | Likely meaning                              | Action                                                                      |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `reached required accuracy`                | Forces below the absolute value of `EDIFFG` | Check the volume change and the stress, then a static run                   |
| The run stopped at step `NSW`              | Relaxation unfinished                       | `cp CONTCAR POSCAR` and continue                                            |
| Steps that use all `NELM` electronic steps | Forces from unconverged densities           | Fix the SCF first (see SCF trouble)                                         |
| Forces oscillate up and down               | Steps too large                             | Reduce `POTIM`; switch `IBRION = 1` to `2` or `3`                           |
| A second `ISIF = 3` run changes the volume | Pulay stress from the old basis             | Raise `ENCUT` to about 1.3 `ENMAX`; repeat until stable <Cite id="S-042" /> |
| `ZBRENT: fatal error in bracketing`        | Conjugate-gradient line search failed       | Restart from `CONTCAR`, tighten `EDIFF`, or use `IBRION = 1`                |
| Temperatures appear in `OSZICAR`           | `IBRION` was not set, so VASP ran MD        | Set `IBRION = 2` explicitly                                                 |

Why judge each run: a clean exit is not a converged structure. Why a force criterion: forces measure distance from the minimum directly. What happens if you skip the checks: every later property is computed on the wrong structure <Cite id="S-041" />.

</WhatChanges>

<Mistakes>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="A positive EDIFFG, often by default">

Cause: `EDIFFG` left out, so the default energy criterion (`EDIFF` times 10) stops the run once steps change the energy little, even with large forces. Fix: a negative `EDIFFG` such as -0.01 or -0.02 <Cite id="S-041" />.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Leaving IBRION out">

Cause: `NSW` above 1 with no `IBRION` defaults to molecular dynamics. Fix: set `IBRION = 2` (or 1, or 3) in every relaxation INCAR <Cite id="S-041" />.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Using ISIF = 3 for a slab or a molecule in a box">

Cause: vacuum has no stress to resist, so the cell shrinks into it. Fix: `ISIF = 2`, and selective dynamics for the layers that stand for the bulk <Cite id="S-041" />.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Trusting one cell relaxation at a low cutoff">

Cause: Pulay stress from a basis fixed at the starting cell. Fix: `ENCUT` about 1.3 times the largest `ENMAX`, and repeat from `CONTCAR` until the cell is stable <Cite id="S-041" /> <Cite id="S-040" />.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Reading the final structure from POSCAR">

Cause: `POSCAR` is the input and is never updated. Fix: the relaxed structure is in `CONTCAR`; the whole path is in `XDATCAR` <Cite id="S-041" />.

</Callout>

</Mistakes>
