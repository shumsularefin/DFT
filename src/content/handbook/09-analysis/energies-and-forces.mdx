<Why>

A finished run gives you dozens of numbers. Only a few of them answer the question you asked. If you quote the wrong energy line, or call a structure relaxed because the energy stopped changing, your result can be off by more than the effect you are studying.

</Why>

<Intuition>

Think of the atoms as balls on a hilly landscape. The **energy** is the height, the **force** on each atom is the slope under it, pointing downhill. A structure is relaxed when every slope is nearly flat. That is why the forces, not only the energy, tell you when to stop.

Two energies appear in OUTCAR. The **free energy** (`free energy TOTEN`) includes a smearing entropy term. The line `energy without entropy` and the `energy(sigma->0)` value next to it do not. For a smearing of ISMEAR = 0 the extrapolated `energy(sigma->0)` is the one the author's handbooks use for comparing energies <Cite id="S-019" />. Whatever you choose, use the same line for every calculation you compare <Cite id="S-041" />.

<Level min="intermediate">

The force on an atom is the negative gradient of the energy with respect to its position. VASP prints it in the `TOTAL-FORCE` block in eV/Angstrom, with a `total drift` line that should be close to zero. A large drift means the forces are not consistent, often from a too-coarse grid or loose electronic convergence <Cite id="S-041" />.

</Level>

<Level min="pro">

Forces are only as accurate as the electronic convergence behind them. A tight force criterion such as EDIFFG = -0.001 needs EDIFF of 1E-7 or tighter <Cite id="S-042" />. Forces from an ionic step that hit NELM are not reliable.

</Level>

</Intuition>

<TheMath>

The force on atom $i$ is $\mathbf{F}_i = -\partial E / \partial \mathbf{R}_i$. The convergence test of a relaxation is $\max_i |\mathbf{F}_i| < |\text{EDIFFG}|$ when EDIFFG is negative <Cite id="S-019" />.

</TheMath>

<InVasp>

Find the energies and the largest force of the last step <Cite id="S-042" />:

<VaspFile
  name="shell"
  code={`grep "energy without entropy" OUTCAR | tail -1
grep -A 30 "TOTAL-FORCE" OUTCAR | tail -35`}
/>

The [relaxation viewer](/handbook/09-analysis/relaxations/) reads the energy and the largest force of every step from an OUTCAR.

</InVasp>

<WhatChanges>

| If you...                                   | ...then                                                     |
| ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Compare F from one run with E0 from another | The difference includes an entropy term, not physics        |
| Use a larger SIGMA                          | The entropy term grows, so the two energy lines drift apart |
| Stop on a tiny energy change                | A flat region can hide forces that are still large          |

Why use the same line everywhere: the entropy term depends on smearing, so mixing lines mixes a numerical choice into your energy difference. What happens if you skip it: a spurious difference of the size of the entropy term.

</WhatChanges>

<Mistakes>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Judging a relaxation by the energy alone">

The energy changes very little near a minimum while forces can still be large. Look at the largest force.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Comparing energies from different smearing settings">

Entropy terms differ with ISMEAR and SIGMA, so energies from different settings are not comparable.

</Callout>

</Mistakes>
