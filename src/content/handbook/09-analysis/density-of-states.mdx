<Why>

The density of states (DOS) counts how many electronic states exist at each energy. It tells you at a glance whether a material is a metal or an insulator, which elements and orbitals (`s`, `p`, `d`, `f`) form the valence and conduction edges, and whether up-spin and down-spin bands are split <Cite id="S-042" />.

</Why>

<Intuition>

Always shift the energy axis so the Fermi level sits at zero ($E - E_F = 0$). States below zero are occupied; states above zero are empty. If there is a clean zero-DOS gap around $E - E_F = 0$, the material is a semiconductor or insulator; if the DOS is non-zero at zero, it is metallic.

With `LORBIT = 11`, `DOSCAR` also provides site- and orbital-projected DOS (PDOS) in the order `s, py, pz, px, dxy, dyz, dz2, dxz, dx2` <Cite id="S-019" />. Keep Gaussian smoothing off when reading band edges, because artificial broadening can smear states into the gap and make an insulator look metallic.

</Intuition>

<Lab id="dos-viewer" title="DOSCAR viewer" />

<TheMath>

The total density of states is the Brillouin-zone sum over bands $n$ and wavevectors $\mathbf k$:

$$D(E) = \sum_{n,\mathbf k} w_{\mathbf k}\,\delta\bigl(E - \varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}\bigr)$$

Integrating $D(E)$ up to $E_F$ recovers the number of valence electrons in the cell <Cite id="S-019" />.

</TheMath>

<InVasp>

- Run a dense k-point mesh with `ISMEAR = -5` (tetrahedron method with Bloechl corrections) for clean, sharp DOS features in insulators and semiconductors <Cite id="S-042" />.
- Set `LORBIT = 11` for $lm$-decomposed PDOS and raise `NEDOS` (e.g. `2000`) for fine energy resolution <Cite id="S-042" />.

</InVasp>

<WhatChanges>

| Setting in DOS run           | Effect on `DOSCAR`                                     |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Dense k-mesh + `ISMEAR = -5` | Sharp van Hove singularities and clean band edges      |
| Coarse k-mesh                | Spiky, noisy DOS peaks                                 |
| Heavy post-smoothing         | Can fill in narrow band gaps artificially              |
| `ISPIN = 2`                  | Separate spin-up and spin-down columns (plot mirrored) |

Why use a denser k-mesh for DOS than for relaxation: DOS resolves sharp energy features across the whole Brillouin zone.

</WhatChanges>

<Mistakes>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Forgetting to subtract E_F before reading the plot">

Raw `DOSCAR` energies have an arbitrary offset that depends on the cell and potential. Always plot against $E - E_F$.

</Callout>

</Mistakes>
