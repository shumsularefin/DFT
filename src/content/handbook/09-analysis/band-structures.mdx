<Why>

A <Term id="band">band</Term> structure plots the allowed electron energies against the crystal momentum $\mathbf k$ along a path of high-symmetry lines through the <Term id="brillouin-zone">Brillouin zone</Term>. The density of states tells you how many states sit at each energy; the band structure also tells you **where in k** they sit. That extra information answers questions the DOS cannot: is the <Term id="band-gap">band gap</Term> direct or indirect, how light or heavy are the electrons and holes (the <Term id="effective-mass">effective mass</Term>), which bands cross, and which atoms and orbitals form the band edges <Cite id="S-042" />.

It is also the plot most often made wrongly. A band run copied from the self-consistent INCAR, a path for the wrong cell, or too few empty bands gives a picture that looks plausible and is wrong <Cite id="S-041" />.

</Why>

<Intuition>

Picture the crystal's electron energies as a landscape over the Brillouin zone. A band structure is a walk along a few straight roads through that landscape, chosen because they pass through the special points ($\Gamma$, X, L, K and so on) where the extremes usually sit. Each road is a segment of the plot; the labels on the horizontal axis are the points you walk through <Cite id="S-006" />.

**Two runs, two jobs.** The density must be converged on a uniform mesh that samples the whole zone. A path does not sample the zone, so the VASP wiki says a line-mode mesh is not suitable for a self-consistent run <Cite id="S-042" />. The workflow is therefore:

1. A self-consistent run on a uniform mesh writes `CHGCAR`.
2. A **non-self-consistent** run reads that density and keeps it fixed (`ICHARG = 11`) while it solves for the eigenvalues at the path points. Nothing about the density changes; only the k-points do <Cite id="S-041" />.

From VASP 6.3 there is a one-run alternative: keep the uniform mesh in `KPOINTS` and put the path in `KPOINTS_OPT`; after the SCF converges, VASP evaluates the bands on the path in the same job <Cite id="S-042" />. Hybrid functionals cannot use `ICHARG = 11` at all; they put the path points into the SCF list with zero weight, as described in [the functionals topic](/handbook/05-practical-choices/functionals/) <Cite id="S-041" />.

**Empty bands.** VASP computes only `NBANDS` bands. The default adds just a few empty bands above the occupied ones, enough for the SCF but often too few to show the conduction band you want to plot <Cite id="S-041" />. Because the density is frozen in the band run, raising `NBANDS` there is safe and cheap.

**Reading the gap.** The top of the filled bands is the valence-band maximum (VBM); the bottom of the empty bands is the conduction-band minimum (CBM). If they sit at the same k-point the gap is **direct**; if not, it is **indirect** and a photon alone cannot bridge it, because light carries almost no crystal momentum. Silicon is the textbook indirect case and GaAs the textbook direct one <Cite id="S-050" />.

**Curvature means mass.** A steep, strongly curved band near an edge means a light, mobile carrier; a flat band means a heavy, localised one <Cite id="S-042" />.

</Intuition>

<TheMath>

The Kohn-Sham band gap is the lowest empty eigenvalue minus the highest filled one, each searched over every k-point and band:

$$E_g = \min_{\mathbf k,\;n \in \text{empty}} \varepsilon_{n\mathbf k} \;-\; \max_{\mathbf k,\;m \in \text{filled}} \varepsilon_{m\mathbf k}$$

The gap is direct when both extremes are found at the same $\mathbf k$.

**Effective mass.** Near a band extremum the energy is close to a parabola in $k$. The curvature of that parabola defines the effective mass <Cite id="S-036" />:

$$\frac{1}{m^*} = \frac{1}{\hbar^2}\,\frac{d^2E}{dk^2}, \qquad \frac{m^*}{m_e} = \frac{\hbar^2/m_e}{d^2E/dk^2}$$

With $E$ in eV and $k$ in $\text{\AA}^{-1}$, the constant is $\hbar^2/m_e = 7.620$ eV $\text{\AA}^2$ (twice the CODATA value $\hbar^2/2m_e = 3.80998$ eV $\text{\AA}^2$) <Cite id="S-031" />.

**Worked example (illustrative numbers, not a real material).** Take three points along a line through a CBM, spaced $\Delta k = 0.02\ \text{\AA}^{-1}$ apart, with energies 0.50381, 0.50000 and 0.50381 eV. The central finite difference gives

$$\frac{d^2E}{dk^2} \approx \frac{E_+ - 2E_0 + E_-}{\Delta k^2} = \frac{0.00762\ \text{eV}}{0.0004\ \text{\AA}^{-2}} = 19.05\ \text{eV}\,\text{\AA}^2,$$

so $m^*/m_e = 7.620 / 19.05 = 0.40$. At a VBM the curvature is negative, so the formula gives a negative mass; the hole mass is its magnitude.

<Level min="intermediate">

Two cautions. First, $k$ must be a Cartesian distance in $\text{\AA}^{-1}$ that includes the factor $2\pi$. `EIGENVAL` lists fractional coordinates of the reciprocal vectors, and VASP prints those vectors without the $2\pi$, so a hand-made $k$ axis can be off by $2\pi$, which changes $m^*$ by a factor of about 39.5 <Cite id="S-019" />. Second, the mass is a tensor: in an anisotropic crystal, or at a valley away from $\Gamma$, the curvature differs by direction, and you should quote the direction. Fit over a few points close to the extremum (a parabola is only valid near it), and check that the result does not change when you halve $\Delta k$. VASPKIT task 912 does this fit on a fine grid around the VBM and CBM <Cite id="S-042" />.

</Level>

<Level min="pro">

The Kohn-Sham gap is not the fundamental gap. Even the exact functional would miss it by the derivative discontinuity of the exchange-correlation potential, and semilocal functionals add self-interaction error on top; both push the gap down <Cite id="S-175" />. This is why PBE gaps are typically too small and why the band dispersion is often more trustworthy than the absolute gap.

**Band unfolding.** A supercell (for a defect, an alloy or a slab) folds the bands of the primitive cell back into its smaller Brillouin zone, so the plot fills with lines. Unfolding projects each supercell state onto the primitive-cell k-points and gives a spectral weight $P_{\mathbf K}(\mathbf k_i) = \sum_n \lvert\langle \mathbf K m \mid \mathbf k_i n\rangle\rvert^2$, the "effective band structure" <Cite id="S-174" />. VASPKIT tasks 281 and 282 prepare the k-points and write the unfolded data <Cite id="S-042" />.

</Level>

</TheMath>

- **Keep the physics identical to the SCF run**: `ENCUT`, `POTCAR`, `ISPIN`, `MAGMOM`, `LMAXMIX` (4 for d, 6 for f elements), the functional and any `LDAU` tags, and the cell itself <Cite id="S-042" />.
- **The path belongs to one cell.** Coordinates depend on the cell setting. Generate them for your exact cell with the [k-path tool](/tools/kpath/), SeeK-path or VASPKIT task 303; if VASPKIT writes a `PRIMCELL.vasp` that differs from your `POSCAR`, use it <Cite id="S-042" /> <Cite id="S-006" />.
- **NBANDS.** Read the SCF value with `grep NBANDS OUTCAR` and raise it in the band run until the conduction bands you need are on the plot. VASP may round it up to suit the parallel layout <Cite id="S-042" />.
- **Fermi level.** Take `E-fermi` from the SCF `OUTCAR`: a value computed from path points alone is unreliable. For semiconductors many people set the VBM to zero instead <Cite id="S-042" /> <Cite id="S-041" />.
- **Route A (VASP 6.3 and newer).** Uniform mesh in `KPOINTS`, path in `KPOINTS_OPT`, no `ICHARG = 11`. The path eigenvalues go to `vasprun.xml` (and `vaspout.h5` in HDF5 builds) <Cite id="S-042" />.
- **Spin.** With `ISPIN = 2`, `EIGENVAL` holds both channels at each k-point (index, E up, E down, occupations). Plot both; the gap is the smallest over both channels, and a half-metal is metallic in one channel and gapped in the other <Cite id="S-041" /> <Cite id="S-042" />.
- **Fat bands.** `LORBIT = 11` writes `PROCAR`, with the weight of every band on each atom and orbital. Drawing each point with a size or colour set by, say, the O p weight shows which orbitals form each band. The weights are sphere projections, so they do not add up to exactly 1 per band <Cite id="S-041" />.
- **Hybrids and meta-GGAs**: no `ICHARG = 11`; use zero-weight path points as in [the functionals topic](/handbook/05-practical-choices/functionals/) <Cite id="S-042" />.
- **Plotting.** VASPKIT task 211 writes `BAND.dat`, `KLABELS` and `BAND_GAP` (213 for projected bands, 911 for the gap, 912 for effective masses) <Cite id="S-042" /> <Cite id="S-020" />. In Python, pymatgen reads `vasprun.xml` and plots bands <Cite id="S-034" />, and py4vasp, the VASP developers' package, reads `vaspout.h5` <Cite id="S-091" />. This site's viewer above reads `EIGENVAL` directly.

</InVasp>

<WhatChanges>

| Choice in the band run         | What you see                                           | Why                                                     |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| `ICHARG = 11` left out         | Jagged bands, wrong gap                                | The density was re-converged on a path                  |
| `CHGCAR` from another cell     | Shifted or distorted bands                             | The fixed density does not belong to this structure     |
| Default `NBANDS`               | Conduction bands cut off at the top of the plot        | VASP adds only a few empty bands                        |
| More `NBANDS`                  | More conduction bands, slightly longer run             | Safe here, because the density is fixed                 |
| `ISMEAR = -5` on a path        | Error or meaningless occupations                       | Tetrahedra need a uniform 3D mesh                       |
| Path for the conventional cell | Labels and folded bands that do not match the textbook | The path is defined for the standardized primitive cell |
| `KPOINTS_OPT` (VASP 6.3+)      | Bands from one run                                     | The path is evaluated after the SCF in the same job     |
| PBE to HSE06                   | Larger gap, similar shape                              | Exact exchange reduces the gap error                    |

Why a separate run: a path cannot converge a density. Why these values: the band run must inherit every physical setting from the SCF. What happens if you get it wrong: a plot that looks like a band structure but describes a different calculation <Cite id="S-041" />.

</WhatChanges>

<Mistakes>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Running the path self-consistently">

Cause: the SCF INCAR was reused without `ICHARG = 11`, so VASP tried to converge the density on path points. Fix: copy the SCF `CHGCAR` into the folder and set `ICHARG = 11`, or use `KPOINTS_OPT` <Cite id="S-041" />.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Changing ISPIN, MAGMOM or LMAXMIX between the SCF and the band run">

Cause: a fresh INCAR for the band run. The frozen density then does not match the Hamiltonian, and the bands are wrong. Fix: copy those tags from the SCF INCAR, and set `LMAXMIX = 4` (d) or `6` (f) in both runs <Cite id="S-042" />.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Reading the gap from a uniform mesh">

Cause: the CBM of an indirect semiconductor can fall between mesh points. Fix: take the gap from the band path or a denser mesh, and check that the path passes through the extremum <Cite id="S-041" />.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="A k axis without the factor 2 pi">

Cause: the k distance was built from fractional coordinates and reciprocal vectors printed without $2\pi$. Effective masses then come out wrong by a large factor. Fix: convert to Cartesian $\text{\AA}^{-1}$ with $2\pi$ included, or let VASPKIT or pymatgen build the axis <Cite id="S-019" />.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Quoting a PBE gap as the material's gap">

Cause: semilocal functionals underestimate gaps. Fix: say which functional gave the number, and use HSE06 or mBJ when the gap itself matters (see [the functionals topic](/handbook/05-practical-choices/functionals/)) <Cite id="S-042" />.

</Callout>

</Mistakes>
