<Why>

In a non-magnetic calculation (`ISPIN = 1`), up and down electrons share one set of orbitals, so any spontaneous magnetic moment is forbidden by construction. Switching to `ISPIN = 2` lets the two spin channels relax independently so the Kohn-Sham solver can lower the total energy whenever exchange stabilization beats the kinetic energy cost of moving electrons into one spin channel <Cite id="S-059" />.

</Why>

<Intuition>

Picture the electron cloud of a magnetic crystal as two overlapping clouds: an up-spin density $n_\uparrow(\mathbf r)$ and a down-spin density $n_\downarrow(\mathbf r)$. Wherever the two clouds match, the local magnetization is zero. Wherever one cloud is denser, that atom carries a net magnetic moment pointing up or down.

Why does a material split its spins at all? Moving electrons from down-spin to up-spin forces them into higher-energy states (a kinetic energy penalty), but electrons of the same spin stay further apart by the Pauli exclusion principle, lowering their exchange energy. In a metal, the competition is captured by the **Stoner criterion**: when the exchange parameter $I$ times the density of states at the Fermi level $N(E_F)$ exceeds $1$, the spin-split state sits lower in energy than the non-magnetic state <Cite id="S-059" />.

The 3D scene below shows a ferromagnetic cubic cell where every magnetic site carries an excess of up-spin density, represented by parallel arrows.

<Scene id="fm-cubic" />

In VASP, `MAGMOM` supplies the initial guess for each atom's moment at step 0, and the SCF loop is free to change both the magnitude and the sign. If you need to test a specific fixed total moment (for example, to map energy versus total spin), `NUPDOWN` constrains the difference between total up and down electrons <Cite id="S-065" />.

</Intuition>

<Lab id="stoner-toy" title="Stoner criterion toy model" />

<TheMath>

In collinear spin-polarized DFT, the Kohn-Sham equations are solved separately for $\sigma \in \{\uparrow, \downarrow\}$ with spin-dependent effective potentials $v_{\text{eff}}^\sigma(\mathbf r)$. The total density and magnetization density are:

$$n(\mathbf r) = n_\uparrow(\mathbf r) + n_\downarrow(\mathbf r), \qquad m(\mathbf r) = n_\uparrow(\mathbf r) - n_\downarrow(\mathbf r)$$

In the constant-DOS Stoner toy model <Cite id="S-059" />, transferring $m/2$ electrons from down-spin to up-spin across a flat band with density of states $N$ per spin changes the energy by:

$$\Delta E(m) = \frac{1}{4N}\bigl(1 - I\,N\bigr) m^2$$

When $I\,N \le 1$, the coefficient is positive and $m = 0$ is the unique minimum. When $I\,N > 1$, the quadratic term turns negative and a spin-split solution with $m \ne 0$ lowers the energy <Cite id="S-059" />.

</TheMath>

<InVasp>

```incar
ISPIN = 2
MAGMOM = 4*4.0 4*0.0
LORBIT = 11
```

- `ISPIN = 2` enables collinear spin polarization (VASP wiki page ISPIN <Cite id="S-019" />).
- `MAGMOM` lists one initial moment per atom in `POSCAR` order. Above, `4*4.0 4*0.0` seeds four magnetic atoms at $4.0\,\mu_B$ and four non-magnetic atoms at $0.0\,\mu_B$ <Cite id="S-042" />.
- `NUPDOWN` fixes the integer difference $N_\uparrow - N_\downarrow$ for the whole cell; leave it unset (default `-1`) for normal unconstrained runs <Cite id="S-065" />.
- Always include `LORBIT = 11` when `ISPIN = 2` so VASP writes per-site moments to `OUTCAR` <Cite id="S-041" />.

</InVasp>

<WhatChanges>

| If you change...                   | ...in the Stoner toy                    | ...in VASP                                                         |
| ---------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Increase $I \cdot N(E_F)$ past $1$ | Minimum moves from $m = 0$ to $m^* > 0$ | Spin-polarized state drops below the non-magnetic energy           |
| `MAGMOM` too small (e.g. `0.1`)    | Not in toy                              | SCF can collapse to $m = 0$ even for a magnetic oxide              |
| Set `NUPDOWN = 0`                  | Forces net $m = 0$                      | Constrains total cell spin (useful for AFM checks, not general FM) |
| Set `ISPIN = 1`                    | Locks $n_\uparrow = n_\downarrow$       | Non-magnetic run; misses spin splitting completely                 |

Why set `ISPIN = 2` with realistic `MAGMOM`: gives SCF a clear symmetry-breaking push toward the physical spin state. What happens if you skip it: magnetic ions converge to a high-energy non-magnetic solution.

</WhatChanges>

<Mistakes>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Forgetting that MAGMOM is only an initial guess">

Setting `MAGMOM = 4*4.0` does not force the final moment to stay at $4.0\,\mu_B$. VASP relaxes the moments during SCF; check `OSZICAR` and `OUTCAR` afterwards.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Leaving NUPDOWN fixed during a ground-state search">

`NUPDOWN` locks the global spin difference of the cell. Use it only when you deliberately want a constrained-moment energy curve, and remove it for unconstrained relaxations.

</Callout>

</Mistakes>
