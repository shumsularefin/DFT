<Why>

After a spin-polarized job finishes, the total energy alone does not tell you which magnetic state the calculation found. A G-type antiferromagnet, a state that collapsed to zero moment and a ferromagnet that the run flipped into can all finish without a warning. You have to read three pieces of output: the total cell moment in `OSZICAR`, the atom-by-atom moments in `OUTCAR`, and, for the electronic structure, the up-spin and down-spin densities of states in `DOSCAR` <Cite id="S-041" /> <Cite id="S-042" />. Each answers a different question, and only together do they tell you what you have.

</Why>

<Intuition>

Think of a building with many flats. The total moment is the building's bill: it tells you how much was used in total, but not by whom. The per-site table is the meter in each flat. Two buildings can have the same total bill (zero) for very different reasons: every flat used nothing, or half the flats produced exactly what the other half used.

1. **`OSZICAR` (`mag=`).** At the end of each ionic step `OSZICAR` prints `mag=` followed by the net <Term id="magnetic-moment">magnetic moment</Term> of the whole cell in Bohr magnetons. For a ferromagnet it is roughly the sum of the site moments. For a clean antiferromagnet it is close to `0.000`, which by itself cannot tell an antiferromagnet from a collapsed non-magnetic state.
2. **`OUTCAR` (`magnetization (x)`).** With `LORBIT = 11`, `OUTCAR` prints a table with the s, p, d (and f) and total moment inside a sphere around each atom, in `POSCAR` order <Cite id="S-041" /> <Cite id="S-042" />. Read the **last** occurrence: a relaxation prints one table per ionic step. An antiferromagnet shows large moments of opposite sign on its magnetic sites (for example `+3.8` and `-3.8`); a collapsed run shows zeros everywhere.
3. **Spin-resolved DOS (`DOSCAR`).** With `ISPIN = 2` the density of states comes in two channels. Plotted with up-spin positive and down-spin negative, a mirror-symmetric picture means zero net polarization, and peaks shifted between the two halves show the exchange splitting <Cite id="S-059" />.

<Scene id="fm-cubic" />

Three facts keep you from misreading the numbers:

- **Site moments are smaller than the formal value.** They are integrated inside spheres around each atom, so part of the moment lies outside. High-spin Fe3+ shows about 4.2 to 4.3 Bohr magnetons, not 5 <Cite id="S-042" />.
- **The site moments do not add up to `mag=`.** The spheres do not fill the cell, so the space between them (the interstitial) carries the rest <Cite id="S-065" />.
- **An integer total moment is a clue.** For an insulator or a half-metal the total cell moment is an integer <Cite id="S-042" />. A half-metal shows a gap at the Fermi level in one spin channel only.

Small moments on non-magnetic atoms (oxygen next to a magnetic ion, say a few hundredths) are induced by their neighbours and are expected.

</Intuition>

<TheMath>

The moment is a count: the number of up-spin electrons minus the number of down-spin electrons, in units of the Bohr magneton. Ten up and eight down give a moment of 2.

<Level min="intermediate">

The total cell moment in `OSZICAR` is the integral over the whole cell $\Omega$ of the spin density, the difference of the up and down densities $n_\uparrow$ and $n_\downarrow$:

$$M_{\text{tot}} = \int_\Omega \bigl(n_\uparrow(\mathbf r) - n_\downarrow(\mathbf r)\bigr)\,d^3r = N_\uparrow - N_\downarrow$$

Each site moment $m_i$ in the `OUTCAR` table is the same integral, restricted to a sphere of radius $R_i$ around ion $i$ at position $\mathbf R_i$:

$$m_i = \int_{\text{sphere } i} \bigl(n_\uparrow(\mathbf r) - n_\downarrow(\mathbf r)\bigr)\,d^3r$$

Because the spheres do not fill $\Omega$, the difference $M_{\text{tot}} - \sum_i m_i = M_{\text{interstitial}}$ is in general not zero <Cite id="S-065" />. In an insulator every band is full or empty in each spin channel, so $N_\uparrow$ and $N_\downarrow$ are both integers and so is $M_{\text{tot}}$; the site moments, being partial integrals, need not be.

</Level>

</TheMath>

<InVasp>

A static SCF on the relaxed structure that writes everything you need to read the magnetism:

<VaspFile
  name="INCAR"
  code={`ISTART  = 1
NSW     = 0
ISPIN   = 2
MAGMOM  = 4.0 -4.0 -4.0 4.0 8*0.0
LORBIT  = 11
NEDOS   = 3001
LMAXMIX = 4
ISMEAR  = 0
SIGMA   = 0.05
EDIFF   = 1E-6`}
  annotations={{
    1: 'start from the relaxation WAVECAR',
    4: 'the same pattern the relaxation used; one value per atom',
    5: 'writes the per-site table and the projected DOS',
    6: 'a fine energy grid for a clean spin-resolved DOS',
  }}
/>

The handbook's commands for a cell of `N` atoms <Cite id="S-042" />:

```bash
grep "mag=" OSZICAR | tail -1                       # total cell moment
grep -A $((N+6)) "magnetization (x)" OUTCAR | tail -$((N+6))   # last per-site table
grep "number of electron" OUTCAR | tail -1          # electrons and total magnetization
```

- Check `OSZICAR` first: every ionic step must have converged electronically before any moment means anything <Cite id="S-041" />.
- In the per-site table, compare the sign and size on each magnetic site with the pattern you seeded. Match rows to atoms by `POSCAR` order.
- For the DOS, VASPkit tasks 111 and 113 read `DOSCAR` and write the total and projected DOS per spin <Cite id="S-042" />. Plot the down channel as negative.
- Drop your `OUTCAR` and `OSZICAR` into the Analyse tab of the [Magnetic configuration tool](/tools/magnetic/) to parse the table and check whether each run kept its intended pattern.

</InVasp>

<WhatChanges>

| Output check          | Healthy FM run                  | Healthy AFM run                      | Collapsed non-magnetic run |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | -------------------------- |
| `OSZICAR` `mag=`      | Large non-zero total            | Near `0.000`                         | `0.000`                    |
| `OUTCAR` site moments | All magnetic sites same sign    | Equal and opposite signs on sites    | All sites near `0.000`     |
| Mirrored DOS          | Up and down shifted differently | Total DOS symmetric, site PDOS split | Up and down identical      |
| Energy vs NM run      | Lower                           | Lower                                | Equal                      |

Why read `OUTCAR` and not only `OSZICAR`: `mag=` is zero for an antiferromagnet and for a collapsed state alike. Why `LORBIT = 11`: without it there is no per-site table. What happens if you skip these checks: an energy from the wrong state enters your comparison.

</WhatChanges>

<Mistakes>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Taking mag = 0 as proof of antiferromagnetism">

Cause: the total moment of an antiferromagnet and of a non-magnetic state are both zero. Fix: read the per-site table; an antiferromagnet has large opposite moments, a collapsed run has none.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Reading the first magnetization table in OUTCAR">

Cause: a relaxation writes a `magnetization (x)` block at every ionic step, and `grep` without `tail` shows the first one. Fix: always take the last block, which belongs to the final structure.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Forgetting LORBIT = 11">

Cause: the tag was left out of the SCF. Without it VASP does not write the per-site table the handbook uses, so you cannot confirm the pattern <Cite id="S-042" />. Fix: add `LORBIT = 11` and rerun the static step from the `WAVECAR`.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Expecting site moments to equal the formal moment">

Cause: Fe3+ is "five unpaired electrons", so 4.2 looks like a failure. The sphere misses part of the moment, so a value somewhat below the formal one is normal <Cite id="S-042" />. Fix: compare with the same quantity in other runs or papers that used the same method, not with the ionic count.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Matching the table to the wrong atoms">

Cause: the table follows `POSCAR` order, and a structure tool reordered the species. Fix: check the species line of the `POSCAR` you actually ran, and match `MAGMOM`, the table and the atoms in that order.

</Callout>

</Mistakes>
