<Why>

Knowing that an atom carries a local magnetic moment is only half the story. Neighbouring moments can align parallel (ferromagnetic, FM) or antiparallel (antiferromagnetic, AFM) in several distinct geometric patterns. A single primitive cell with one magnetic atom can only represent FM, because periodic boundary conditions copy that one arrow into every neighbouring cell. To test antiferromagnetic states, you must build a commensurate supercell that fits the repeating spin pattern <Cite id="S-058" />.

</Why>

<Intuition>

On a simple cubic or pseudocubic perovskite B-site sublattice, each magnetic ion has 6 nearest magnetic neighbours. Wollan and Koehler <Cite id="S-058" /> named the four classic collinear orderings by how the signs alternate along the three lattice axes:

- **FM (ferromagnetic):** all 6 nearest neighbours are parallel. Net cell moment is non-zero.
- **A-type AFM:** ferromagnetic planes stacked antiferromagnetically along one axis (4 parallel, 2 antiparallel nearest neighbours).
- **C-type AFM:** antiferromagnetic in the plane, ferromagnetic chains along the axis (2 parallel, 4 antiparallel nearest neighbours).
- **G-type AFM:** every nearest neighbour is antiparallel in all three directions (0 parallel, 6 antiparallel nearest neighbours).

<TheMath>

A collinear ordering with propagation vector $\mathbf k$ assigns each magnetic site at lattice translation $\mathbf R_j$ the sign:

$$s_j = \cos(2\pi\,\mathbf k \cdot \mathbf R_j) \in \{+1, -1\}$$

For a simple cubic magnetic sublattice (one site per primitive cell):

- FM: $\mathbf k = (0, 0, 0)$ (determinant 1).
- A-type: $\mathbf k = (0, 0, \tfrac{1}{2})$ (minimal supercell determinant 2).
- C-type: $\mathbf k = (\tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac{1}{2}, 0)$ (minimal supercell determinant 2).
- G-type: $\mathbf k = (\tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac{1}{2})$ (minimal supercell determinant 2).

To host FM, A, C and G together inside **one common supercell** (so all four runs share one lattice and one k-point mesh), the minimal common supercell for the cubic perovskite has determinant 4 (four formula units) <Cite id="S-058" />.

</TheMath>

<WhatChanges>

| Ordering        | Parallel / antiparallel NN (cubic B-site) | Net moment | Minimal cell size (perovskite) |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ---------- | ------------------------------ |
| FM              | 6 parallel, 0 antiparallel                | Non-zero   | 1 formula unit                 |
| A-type AFM      | 4 parallel, 2 antiparallel                | Zero       | 2 formula units                |
| C-type AFM      | 2 parallel, 4 antiparallel                | Zero       | 2 formula units                |
| G-type AFM      | 0 parallel, 6 antiparallel                | Zero       | 2 formula units                |
| Common cell all | All four patterns fit in one cell         | Depends    | 4 formula units                |

Why build a supercell: a 1-atom magnetic cell cannot represent an AFM pattern. What happens if you skip it: you only ever test FM and can miss a lower-energy AFM ground state.

</WhatChanges>

<Mistakes>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Putting opposite signs in a cell that is too small">

If a cell has only one magnetic atom, you cannot write an antiferromagnetic `MAGMOM`. Build a commensurate supercell first, or check whether an orthorhombic cell (like _Pnma_ with 4 B-sites) already fits A, C and G without enlargement.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Forgetting POSCAR species ordering in MAGMOM">

VASP groups atoms by species in `POSCAR`. If your `POSCAR` lists 4 Sr, 4 Mn, 12 O, the 4 magnetic entries must sit in positions 5 to 8, not positions 1 to 4.

</Callout>

</Mistakes>
