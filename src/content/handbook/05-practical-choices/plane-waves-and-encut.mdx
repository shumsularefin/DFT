<Why>

ENCUT is the first number you choose in every INCAR, and the one you are most often asked about in a group meeting. It decides how finely VASP can describe the electrons, how long the run takes and whether a cell relaxation can be trusted. This page explains what it controls, why the author's protocol sets it to 1.3 times the largest ENMAX, and what goes wrong otherwise.

</Why>

<Intuition>

In a crystal the pattern repeats from cell to cell, so a natural way to describe an orbital is as a sum of waves that repeat with the cell: <Term id="plane-wave">plane waves</Term>. A few long waves capture the broad shape; short waves add fine detail. This is the same idea as building a sound from a fundamental tone and its overtones.

Short waves carry more kinetic energy. <Tag name="ENCUT" /> is a kinetic-energy cutoff in eV: VASP keeps every plane wave below it and throws away the rest <Cite id="S-010" />. In reciprocal space the kept waves fill a sphere, which the lab draws. Raise ENCUT and the sphere grows, so the description gets finer and the run gets slower.

Three things follow.

- **The energy converges from above as ENCUT rises.** More waves can only lower the energy, so a converged ENCUT is one where raising it further changes the energy by less than your target.
- **Energy differences converge faster than energies.** Errors largely cancel when you compare two structures computed with the same ENCUT, which is why the protocol says to keep it identical in every calculation you compare <Cite id="S-042" />.
- **Stress converges much more slowly than energy.** When a cell changes volume during a relaxation, the set of plane waves is kept fixed in number while the sphere changes, and the stress picks up an artificial part called <Term id="pulay-stress">Pulay stress</Term> <Cite id="S-040" />. The cure is a higher cutoff.

**Why 1.3 times ENMAX.** Every POTCAR states a recommended cutoff, ENMAX (`grep ENMAX POTCAR`). The VASP wiki notes the default cutoff often has to be raised by about 30 % to converge the stress tensor, so the author's protocol uses ENCUT = 1.3 times the largest ENMAX among your species for relaxations, and the same value in every later run <Cite id="S-042" /> <Cite id="S-019" />. It is a rule of thumb that you still check with a convergence test (module 8).

<Level min="intermediate">

The number of plane waves grows roughly as ENCUT to the power 3/2 times the cell volume, because the kept waves fill a sphere whose radius grows as the square root of ENCUT <Cite id="S-049" />. The lab counts them exactly for the cell you choose.

</Level>

<Level min="pro">

Pulay stress is nearly isotropic and always pushes the same way: at a fixed number of plane waves the basis gets worse as the volume grows, so the cell is pulled towards too small a volume. Restarting a volume relaxation rebuilds the basis for the new cell and reduces the error, and a higher cutoff makes it small in the first place <Cite id="S-040" /> <Cite id="S-041" />.

</Level>

</Intuition>

<Lab id="encut-sphere" title="The plane-wave sphere" />

<TheMath>

An orbital at wavevector $\mathbf{k}$ is written as $\psi_{\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{\mathbf{G}} c_{\mathbf{G}}\, e^{i(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{G})\cdot\mathbf{r}}$, a sum over reciprocal lattice vectors $\mathbf{G}$. The cutoff keeps the terms with

$$\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}|\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{G}|^2 < E_\text{cut}$$

<Cite id="S-010" />. The number of kept waves is about $\frac{V}{6\pi^2}\left(\frac{2m E_\text{cut}}{\hbar^2}\right)^{3/2}$ for a cell of volume $V$ <Cite id="S-049" />.

</TheMath>

<WhatChanges>

| If you...                                        | ...then                                                        |
| ------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Raise ENCUT                                      | More plane waves; lower and better converged energy; slower    |
| Use ENMAX instead of 1.3 x ENMAX in a cell relax | Pulay stress: the relaxed volume comes out too small           |
| Compare energies computed with different ENCUT   | The difference contains a basis-set error, not only physics    |
| Mix POTCARs with different ENMAX                 | Use the largest one, or the hardest element is under-described |

Why this value: 1.3 x ENMAX converges the stress for relaxations. What happens if you skip it: energies may look fine while the volume and every property that depends on it are off.

</WhatChanges>

<Mistakes>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Changing ENCUT between the relaxation and the later runs">

Keep it identical in every calculation you compare <Cite id="S-042" />.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Treating 1.3 x ENMAX as converged without checking">

It is a rule of thumb. Module 8 shows how to test it for your system and property.

</Callout>

</Mistakes>
