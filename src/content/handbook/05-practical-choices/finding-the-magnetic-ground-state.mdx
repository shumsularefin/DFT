<Why>

Magnetic energy differences between FM, A-, C- and G-type states are often only a few to a few tens of meV per magnetic ion <Cite id="S-060" />. That is smaller than the k-point sampling error between two differently shaped cells, and small enough that a collapsed moment or an inconsistent `ENCUT` can flip the ranking completely. A reliable ground-state search needs strict apples-to-apples comparisons.

</Why>

<Intuition>

The standard recipe has four steps:

1. **Use one common supercell** that hosts every ordering you plan to compare, with the exact same atom order, `ENCUT`, functional, smearing and k-point mesh.
2. **Run each ordering** from its own `MAGMOM` line (all other tags identical) with `LORBIT = 11`.
3. **Verify that every run kept its intended pattern** by checking the final per-site moments in `OUTCAR`. If an AFM run collapsed to non-magnetic or flipped into FM, its energy does not represent the intended state.
4. **Compare total energies per magnetic ion** (typically in meV per magnetic ion relative to FM or the lowest state).

The author's Workflow Handbook compares the **energy without entropy** (from `OUTCAR`) **per formula unit** instead, and calls differences below a few meV per formula unit within DFT uncertainty <Cite id="S-042" />. Both give the same ranking, because they divide the same energies by different counts. Say which one you use when you report the numbers. The [magnetic step of the Workflow](/workflow/magnetic/) follows the per-formula-unit convention.

<Scene id="afm-g" />

Once you have verified energies for enough distinct orderings, you can also map them onto a Heisenberg spin model to extract nearest-neighbour exchange constants $J_1, J_2$ in the Analyse tab of the [Magnetic configuration tool](/tools/magnetic/) <Cite id="S-060" />.

</Intuition>

<TheMath>

For a cell containing $N_{\text{mag}}$ magnetic ions, the energy difference relative to the ferromagnetic state is:

$$\Delta E_{\text{mag}} = \frac{E_{\text{ordering}} - E_{\text{FM}}}{N_{\text{mag}}}$$

When fitting a classical Heisenberg Hamiltonian, the energy of each configuration is linear in the exchange constants $J_n$:

$$E_{\text{config}} = E_0 + \sum_n c_n(\text{config})\,J_n$$

where $c_n$ is determined by the parallel and antiparallel neighbour counts in shell $n$ <Cite id="S-060" />. Because different papers use different sign conventions, pair-counting factors (once vs twice) and spin normalization (unit vectors vs $|S|$), always write out the exact Hamiltonian convention alongside every reported $J_n$.

</TheMath>

<InVasp>

```incar
ISPIN = 2
MAGMOM = 2*4.0 2*-4.0 16*0.0
LORBIT = 11
LMAXMIX = 4
```

- Keep `ENCUT`, `KPOINTS`, `ISMEAR`, `SIGMA`, `PREC`, `LASPH` and `LDAU*` identical across every ordering in the comparison <Cite id="S-042" />.
- Set `LMAXMIX = 4` for 3d transition metals (and `6` for f-electrons) so charge-density mixing handles localized orbitals properly <Cite id="S-042" />.
- Remember that `DFT+U` (`LDAUU`) often changes both the magnitude of the moments and the preferred magnetic ordering; report the $U_{\text{eff}}$ used <Cite id="S-014" />.
- Do not mix relaxed cells from one ordering with unrelaxed cells from another unless you are deliberately comparing fully relaxed energies at matched convergence <Cite id="S-065" />.

</InVasp>

<WhatChanges>

| Pitfall                      | Symptom in output                        | Fix                                                                          |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Different cells per ordering | Spurious meV shifts from k-mesh mismatch | Run all orderings in one `commonSupercell`                                   |
| Moment collapse              | `OUTCAR` site moments near $0\,\mu_B$    | Increase initial `MAGMOM` (e.g. 4 to 5 $\mu_B$) or check `LDAU`              |
| Symmetry trap                | SCF stays in a higher-symmetry state     | Check that `MAGMOM` actually breaks symmetry; verify final signs             |
| Changing $U_{\text{eff}}$    | Ordering preference flips (e.g. FM to G) | Test sensitivity to $U_{\text{eff}}$ and cite the choice <Cite id="S-014" /> |

Why compare in one cell: cancels k-point and basis-set differences down to the sub-meV level. What happens if you skip it: numerical noise can masquerade as a magnetic phase transition.

</WhatChanges>

<Mistakes>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Ranking energies without checking final site moments">

A calculation seeded as G-type AFM can converge to FM or non-magnetic. Always inspect the `magnetization (x)` table in `OUTCAR` before accepting an energy into the ranking.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Reporting a Heisenberg J without its Hamiltonian">

Two papers on the same crystal can report $J$ values that differ in sign and by a factor of 2 or $S^2$. Always state the Hamiltonian form explicitly.

</Callout>

</Mistakes>
