<Why>

Transition-metal oxides, many magnets and rare-earth compounds are where plain GGA often fails badly: band gaps vanish, moments are too small, oxidation states come out wrong. DFT+U is the cheap, widely used fix. It has one adjustable number, and you need to know where that number comes from and what it does before you trust a result that depends on it.

</Why>

<Intuition>

Recall the self-interaction error (module 4): in LDA and GGA an electron partly repels itself. For spread-out s and p electrons this matters little. For d and f electrons, which sit close to their atom, it pushes them to spread over neighbours and to take fractional occupations, as if half an electron sat in an orbital.

DFT+U adds a penalty that is zero when an orbital is empty or full and largest when it is half filled <Cite id="S-014" />. Electrons are then pushed to commit: occupied d states move down in energy, empty ones up, and a gap can open. The lab shows the penalty and its potential for one orbital.

The strength is **U_eff = U - J** in eV, set per element. It is not computed by the standard method: it is either taken from the literature for a similar system, fitted to an experimental property, or computed with a separate linear-response calculation <Cite id="S-042" />. The same U must be used in every calculation you compare, which matters for formation energies.

<Level min="intermediate">

In VASP the simplified (Dudarev) form is `LDAUTYPE = 2`, where only U - J matters. `LDAUL` says which orbital gets the correction per species (2 for d, 3 for f, -1 for none), `LDAUU` and `LDAUJ` give U and J per species in POSCAR order <Cite id="S-019" />. Set `LMAXMIX = 4` (d) or 6 (f) so the occupations survive in the mixer and the CHGCAR <Cite id="S-042" />.

</Level>

<Level min="pro">

DFT+U energies depend on U, so formation and reaction energies that mix +U and non-+U compounds need a correction scheme (for example the Materials Project mixing scheme). DFT+U can also have several self-consistent solutions with different orbital occupations; the loop may land in a metastable one, so check occupations with `LDAUPRINT = 2` and try different starting points <Cite id="S-042" /> <Cite id="S-049" />.

</Level>

</Intuition>

<Lab id="dft-plus-u" title="The DFT+U penalty" />

<TheMath>

In the Dudarev form the correction for one atom is

$$E_U = \frac{U_\text{eff}}{2}\sum_{\sigma}\Big[\text{Tr}\,\rho^\sigma - \text{Tr}\big(\rho^\sigma\rho^\sigma\big)\Big]$$

where $\rho^\sigma$ is the occupation matrix of the localized orbitals for spin $\sigma$ <Cite id="S-014" />. For a single orbital with occupation $n$ this is $E_U = \tfrac{U_\text{eff}}{2}\, n(1-n)$, and the potential it adds is $V = \partial E_U/\partial n = U_\text{eff}\,(\tfrac{1}{2} - n)$. For $U_\text{eff} = 4$ eV and $n = 0.5$, $E_U = 0.5$ eV; for $n = 1$, $V = -2$ eV.

</TheMath>

<WhatChanges>

| If you...                            | ...then                                                           |
| ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Raise U_eff                          | Occupied d states move down, empty ones up; the gap usually grows |
| Use different U in compared runs     | Energy differences are meaningless                                |
| Forget LMAXMIX = 4 for d elements    | Occupations lost in the mixer; slow or wrong convergence          |
| Give LDAUL, LDAUU in the wrong order | U lands on the wrong element                                      |

Why a penalty: it removes some self-interaction where it hurts most. What happens if you pick U to match the property you then "predict": the prediction is circular.

</WhatChanges>

<Mistakes>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Tuning U to the band gap and then claiming the gap as a result">

If U was fitted to the gap, the gap is an input. Say so.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="LDAU lists per atom instead of per species">

`LDAUL`, `LDAUU` and `LDAUJ` take one value per species (per POTCAR), not per atom. That is the opposite of MAGMOM.

</Callout>

</Mistakes>
