<Why>

Before spending computer time on a family of oxides, geometric descriptors help you decide which compositions can plausibly adopt the perovskite framework and how much octahedral tilting to expect <Cite id="S-001" /> <Cite id="S-002" />.

</Why>

<Intuition>

<Callout variant="note" title="Will be updated: results come from ongoing research">

This worked example documents the complete `RCuO3` workflow and input protocol. Computed DFT result numbers will be added after the research is published. Until then this page shows the method and the inputs only; no result on it is a real or invented number.

</Callout>

An $AB\mathrm{O}_3$ perovskite consists of a three-dimensional network of corner-sharing $B\mathrm{O}_6$ octahedra with larger $A$-site cations sitting in the 12-coordinated cavities between them <Cite id="S-001" />. In the $R\mathrm{CuO}_3$ series, stepping across the lanthanide row $R^{3+}$ systematically shrinks the $A$-site Shannon ionic radius, driving progressive octahedral tilting while keeping the formal copper oxidation state fixed <Cite id="S-003" />.

<Scene id="perovskite-octahedra" />

Use the <a href="/tools/tolerance-factor/">Tolerance-factor explorer</a> to compare Goldschmidt $t$, the octahedral factor $\mu$, and Bartel's $\tau$ across the $R\mathrm{CuO}_3$ series. Remember that Shannon ionic radii depend on both oxidation state and coordination number (12-fold for $A$, 6-fold for $B$ and $\mathrm{O}^{2-}$), and missing CN entries are reported honestly as gaps rather than guessed <Cite id="S-003" />.

</Intuition>

<TheMath>

Goldschmidt's tolerance factor and the octahedral factor are defined from the Shannon radii $r_A$, $r_B$, and $r_X$ <Cite id="S-001" /> <Cite id="S-002" />:

$$t = \frac{r_A + r_X}{\sqrt{2}\,(r_B + r_X)}, \qquad \mu = \frac{r_B}{r_X}$$

Values of $t$ near unity favor cubic symmetry, while smaller $t$ indicates stronger tilting of the $B\mathrm{O}_6$ octahedra. Because tolerance factors are empirical heuristics, they guide candidate selection but never replace a full DFT stability check <Cite id="S-002" />.

</TheMath>

<InVasp>

- Screen candidate $R^{3+}$ ions in the <a href="/tools/tolerance-factor/">Tolerance-factor explorer</a> before building supercells.
- Record the exact oxidation states and coordination numbers used for every radius lookup <Cite id="S-003" />.
- Carry the selected composition directly into the <a href="/builder/">Material Builder</a> or <a href="/tools/input-builder/">Input Builder</a>.

</InVasp>

<WhatChanges>

| $A$-site radius trend               | Geometric effect on $B\mathrm{O}_6$ network        | Impact on DFT setup                                                      |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Smaller $R^{3+}$ across lanthanides | Lower Goldschmidt $t$, stronger octahedral tilting | Orthorhombic supercell (e.g. $Pnma$) needed instead of 5-atom cubic cell |
| Fixed $\mathrm{Cu}^{3+}$ $B$-site   | Constant octahedral factor $\mu$                   | Same $B$-site PAW potential and $d$-orbital considerations               |

Why screen first: geometric screening narrows a large rare-earth series to a prioritized shortlist before burning cluster hours.

</WhatChanges>

<Mistakes>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Treating tolerance factors as proof of thermodynamic stability">

A favorable Goldschmidt $t$ or Bartel $\tau$ only indicates geometric compatibility with the perovskite framework. Decomposition into competing binary oxides must still be tested with DFT total energies.

</Callout>

</Mistakes>
