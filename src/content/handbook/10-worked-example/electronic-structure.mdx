<Why>

The electronic structure is where the chemistry of $\mathrm{Cu}^{3+}$ becomes visible. The <Term id="density-of-states">density of states</Term> shows whether there is a <Term id="band-gap">band gap</Term> at the <Term id="fermi-level">Fermi level</Term>, and its projections show which states sit there: Cu $3d$, O $2p$ or a mixture. For a compound with a small or negative <Term id="charge-transfer-energy">charge-transfer energy</Term> the answer is expected to be a mixture, and that is the physics this project wants to follow across the rare-earth series <Cite id="S-098" /> <Cite id="S-099" />. The plots also give a hard test of the model, because the fully oxidized compound is reported to be metallic <Cite id="S-096" />: a calculation that finds a wide gap is telling you something about its settings, not about the material.

</Why>

<Intuition>

<Callout variant="note" title="Will be updated: results come from ongoing research">

This page describes how the DOS, the projected DOS and the band structure of `RCuO3` are computed and read. Band gaps, orbital characters and moments from the author's runs will be added after the research is published. Until then this page shows the method and the inputs only; no plot or number on it is a real or invented result.

</Callout>

**Three runs, one geometry.** Everything here uses the relaxed structure and the magnetic state with the lowest energy from the relaxation page. The author's protocol has three steps after the relaxation <Cite id="S-042" />:

1. **Static SCF** in the same folder as the relaxation: no ionic steps, a converged uniform mesh, `LORBIT = 11` for projections and a fine energy grid. It gives the energy you report, the `DOSCAR` and the `CHGCAR`.
2. **Density of states** from that `DOSCAR`, total and projected on Cu $d$ and O $p$.
3. **Band structure** along a path through the high-symmetry points, computed from the converged density.

**What the DOS tells you.** In the cubic picture the five Cu $3d$ orbitals split into three $t_{2g}$ and two $e_g$ orbitals, and only the $e_g$ orbitals point at the oxygen neighbours <Cite id="S-100" />. With eight $d$ electrons the $t_{2g}$ set is full and the $e_g$ set holds two. So look for three things near the Fermi level: whether the DOS there is zero (gap) or not (metal); whether the states are mostly Cu $e_g$, mostly O $2p$ or both; and whether spin up and spin down differ (a moment).

**What the bands add.** The DOS counts states; the bands show where in the <Term id="brillouin-zone">Brillouin zone</Term> they are. A band crossing the Fermi level shows a metal directly. A broad band with mixed Cu and O character means strong covalent mixing; a flat band near the Fermi level means localized states, which are the ones most sensitive to $U$.

**Keep the settings identical.** The band run reads the density of the SCF. If `ISPIN`, `MAGMOM`, `LMAXMIX` or the `LDAU` tags differ between the two, the band run starts from a density it was not made for, and the eigenvalues are wrong without any error message <Cite id="S-042" />.

</Intuition>

<TheMath>

The density of states counts the Kohn-Sham states per energy interval, summed over bands $n$ and k-points $\mathbf k$ with weights $w_\mathbf k$:

$$g(E) = \sum_{n,\mathbf k} w_\mathbf k\, \delta(E - \varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}).$$

In practice each delta function is broadened, here by the Gaussian of `ISMEAR = 0` with width `SIGMA`. The projected DOS weights each state by how much of it lies in orbital $lm$ inside the sphere around atom $I$:

$$g_{I,lm}(E) = \sum_{n,\mathbf k} w_\mathbf k\, \lvert\langle Y_{lm}^I \mid \psi_{n\mathbf k}\rangle\rvert^2\, \delta(E - \varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}).$$

With `LORBIT = 11` VASP writes these projections per atom and per $lm$ to `DOSCAR` and `PROCAR` <Cite id="S-019" />. Because the spheres do not fill the cell, the projections do not add up to the total DOS; compare shapes and relative weights, not absolute sums.

**The cubic path.** For the simple cubic Brillouin zone the special points are $\Gamma = (0,0,0)$, $X = (0,\tfrac12,0)$, $M = (\tfrac12,\tfrac12,0)$ and $R = (\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$ in units of the reciprocal lattice vectors, and the Setyawan-Curtarolo path is $\Gamma$-X-M-$\Gamma$-R-X, then M-R <Cite id="S-005" />. The doubled cell has a different Brillouin zone, so its path has different labels, and its bands are the cubic bands folded back into a smaller zone.

<Level min="intermediate">

**Why `NEDOS` and `SIGMA` matter.** The energy grid of `DOSCAR` has `NEDOS` points between the lowest and highest eigenvalue. With 3001 points the grid spacing is well below `SIGMA = 0.05`, so the broadening, not the grid, sets the resolution <Cite id="S-042" />. A wide `SIGMA` can fill in a small gap; a mesh that is too coarse gives a spiky DOS. Both are checked by repeating the DOS on a denser mesh.

**The gap in a DFT calculation.** A Kohn-Sham gap is not the measured gap, and semilocal functionals usually underestimate it. Adding $U$ on the Cu $d$ orbitals pushes occupied and empty $d$ states apart and can open a gap. For a compound reported to be a metal, a gap that appears only above some value of $U$ is a warning about that value, not a prediction <Cite id="S-096" />.

</Level>

<Level min="pro">

For a charge-transfer compound the states at the top of the valence band are largely O $2p$, and a hole in them (written $d^9\underline{L}$, with $\underline{L}$ a ligand hole) can be lower in energy than the nominal $d^8$ <Cite id="S-098" /> <Cite id="S-099" />. In the PDOS this would show up as strong O $p$ weight in the empty states just above the Fermi level. The `LORBIT = 11` moments of Cu can then come out smaller than the nominal 2 $\mu_B$ of the high-spin $d^8$ picture, partly because part of the moment sits on oxygen and partly because the sphere does not catch all of it <Cite id="S-041" />.

</Level>

</TheMath>

<InVasp>

**Step 1: the static SCF.** In the folder of the final relaxation, copy `CONTCAR` to `POSCAR` and use this INCAR. The example is for the ten-atom cell with the G-type state; use the `MAGMOM` of whichever state had the lowest energy, and the same `LDAU` block if you use $+U$.

<VaspFile
  name="INCAR"
  code={`SYSTEM   = LaCuO3 static SCF for DOS and bands
PREC     = Accurate
ENCUT    = 520
EDIFF    = 1E-6
ALGO     = Normal
NELM     = 120
ISTART   = 1
ISMEAR   = 0
SIGMA    = 0.05
KSPACING = 0.2
NSW      = 0
IBRION   = -1
ISPIN    = 2
MAGMOM   = 2*0 3 -3 6*0
LORBIT   = 11
NEDOS    = 3001
LASPH    = .TRUE.
LMAXMIX  = 4
LREAL    = .FALSE.
LCHARG   = .TRUE.
LWAVE    = .TRUE.`}
  annotations={{
    7: 'reuse WAVECAR from the relaxation in this folder',
    10: 'the converged spacing; no KPOINTS file in the folder',
    14: 'same order as POSCAR: La La Cu Cu then six O',

    16: 'fine energy grid for DOSCAR',

/>

Before you look at any plot, check the run: `EDIFF` reached in `OSZICAR`, the total moment and the site moments as on the relaxation page, and the smearing entropy small. The protocol tightens `EDIFF` to `1E-6` for this step if a looser value was used before <Cite id="S-042" />.

**Step 2: the DOS.**

1. Total and spin-resolved DOS: load `DOSCAR` into the DOSCAR viewer on the [density of states](/handbook/09-analysis/density-of-states/) page. It plots spin up and spin down, shifted so that the Fermi level is at zero.
2. Projections: use VASPKIT's DOS options (111 and 113 in the author's protocol) to extract the total and projected DOS <Cite id="S-042" />, then plot Cu $d$ (with $d_{z^2}$ and $d_{x^2-y^2}$ for $e_g$) against O $p$.

**Step 3: the bands.** Get the path from the <a href="/tools/kpath/">k-path tool</a> with your relaxed `POSCAR`; it writes a line-mode `KPOINTS`, `KPATH.in` and `KPOINTS_OPT`. Then use one of the two routes of the protocol <Cite id="S-042" />:

- **Route A (VASP 6.3 or newer):** put the path in `KPOINTS_OPT` next to the uniform mesh and rerun the SCF. VASP converges on the mesh and then evaluates the bands on the path <Cite id="S-019" />.
- **Route B:** in a copy of the folder with `CHGCAR`, set `ICHARG = 11`, put the line-mode path in `KPOINTS` (and remove `KSPACING`), and set `LCHARG = .FALSE.`. Keep every other line, `LMAXMIX` and `MAGMOM` included.

<VaspFile
  name="shell"
  code={`mkdir bands && cp POSCAR POTCAR CHGCAR INCAR bands/
cp KPOINTS.path bands/KPOINTS      # line-mode file saved from the k-path tool
cd bands
sed -i -e "/^ *KSPACING/d" -e "s/^ *ISTART.*/ICHARG   = 11/" \\
       -e "s/^ *LCHARG.*/LCHARG   = .FALSE./" INCAR
mpirun vasp_std > vasp.out
vaspkit   # option 211: band data per spin channel`}
/>

Read the result in the band viewer on the [band structures](/handbook/09-analysis/band-structures/) page or from the VASPKIT output. For a spin-polarized run, plot spin up and spin down separately.

**Questions to answer from the plots.** Is the DOS at the Fermi level zero or not, in each spin channel? How much of the weight near the Fermi level is Cu $e_g$ and how much is O $p$? Do the bands crossing the Fermi level (if any) disperse strongly? And do the answers change between $U = 0$ and the values of your $U$ scan?

</InVasp>

<WhatChanges>

| Setting or choice            | What it changes                                | What to watch                                  |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| `LORBIT = 11`                | Writes per-atom, per-$lm$ projections          | Cu $e_g$ against O $p$ near the Fermi level    |
| `NEDOS` larger               | Finer energy grid in `DOSCAR`                  | Grid spacing below `SIGMA`                     |
| `SIGMA` smaller, denser mesh | Sharper DOS                                    | Whether a small gap opens or closes            |
| `ICHARG = 11` band run       | Eigenvalues on the path from the fixed density | `ISPIN`, `MAGMOM`, `LMAXMIX`, `LDAU` unchanged |
| `KPOINTS_OPT` instead        | Bands in the same run as the SCF               | Needs VASP 6.3 or newer                        |
| $U$ on Cu $d$ raised         | Moves occupied and empty $d$ states apart      | A gap opening against the reported metal       |

Why this order: the DOS and bands are only as good as the density they come from, so the SCF comes first and is checked first. Why projections: the question is about orbital character, which the total DOS cannot show. What happens if you skip the checks: a plot from a run that hit `NELM`, or flipped its moments, looks just as convincing as a correct one.

</WhatChanges>

<Mistakes>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Changing LMAXMIX or the spin setup for the band run">

The `ICHARG = 11` run reads a `CHGCAR` written with $d$ components and spin. If `LMAXMIX`, `ISPIN` or `MAGMOM` differ, that information is lost and the bands are wrong. Fix: copy the SCF INCAR and change only `ICHARG`, the k-points and `LCHARG` <Cite id="S-042" />.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Reading the gap off a wide smearing">

With a large `SIGMA` the Gaussian tails fill a small gap and the DOS at the Fermi level looks nonzero. Fix: keep `SIGMA = 0.05` or smaller, use a dense mesh, and confirm a gap or its absence from the band eigenvalues.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Leaving KSPACING in the band run">

With `KSPACING` in the INCAR and no `KPOINTS` file, VASP builds a uniform mesh instead of the path. Fix: remove `KSPACING` and put the line-mode `KPOINTS` in the folder <Cite id="S-041" />.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Choosing U so that the result looks like an insulator">

Tuning $U$ until a gap opens fits the model to an expectation, and for this compound it contradicts the reported metallic behaviour <Cite id="S-096" />. Fix: show the DOS across your $U$ scan and state which value you use and why.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Adding up projections and expecting the total">

The projections cover only the spheres around the atoms, so their sum is smaller than the total DOS. Fix: compare projected curves with each other and use the total DOS for counting states.

</Callout>

</Mistakes>
