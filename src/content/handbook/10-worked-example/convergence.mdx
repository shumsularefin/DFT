<Why>

Perovskite oxides contain oxygen with a hard PAW potential, so running at an unconverged plane-wave cutoff or sparse k-mesh biases lattice constants, tilt angles, and relative phase energies <Cite id="S-042" />.

</Why>

<Intuition>

<Callout variant="note" title="Results pending: author data not yet supplied">

This page lays out the convergence testing protocol for `RCuO3`. Converged `ENCUT` and k-mesh values from the author's VASP runs will be added once the output files are placed in `data/author/`.

</Callout>

Hold the cell fixed and run a clean single-point (`IBRION = -1`, `NSW = 0`) sweep over `ENCUT` first, then sweep the Gamma-centered k-point mesh at the chosen cutoff <Cite id="S-042" />. Paste the resulting `OSZICAR` energies into the <a href="/tools/convergence/">Convergence Plotter</a> to check when the energy per atom settles below your target threshold.

<Scene id="reciprocal-fcc" />

Always test convergence on the quantity you care about most: energy differences between polymorphs or magnetic orderings often converge faster than raw total energies, while stresses require higher cutoffs <Cite id="S-019" />.

</Intuition>

<TheMath>

Convergence is tracked by the energy difference per atom relative to the tightest tested setting:

$$\Delta E_i = \frac{|E_i - E_{\mathrm{ref}}|}{N_{\mathrm{atoms}}}$$

A common research target for bulk oxides is $\Delta E_i < 1\text{ meV/atom}$, verified explicitly rather than assumed <Cite id="S-042" />.

</TheMath>

<InVasp>

- Generate the `ENCUT` and k-mesh series folders directly from the <a href="/tools/input-builder/">Input Builder</a>.
- Keep `EDIFF = 1E-6` or tighter so electronic noise does not mask small convergence steps <Cite id="S-042" />.
- Plot the series in <a href="/tools/convergence/">Convergence Plotter</a> and record the chosen parameters in your lab notebook.

</InVasp>

<WhatChanges>

| Parameter swept | What it controls                    | Sign of convergence                                   |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| `ENCUT`         | Plane-wave basis completeness       | Energy per atom and pressure plateau smoothly         |
| k-mesh density  | Brillouin-zone integration accuracy | Energy per atom settles and stays flat on finer grids |

Why sweep one at a time: changing `ENCUT` and k-mesh simultaneously hides which parameter caused an energy shift.

</WhatChanges>

<Mistakes>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Using different k-meshes for competing phases">

When comparing cubic and orthorhombic supercells, scale the k-mesh inversely with each lattice vector length so the reciprocal-space spacing stays matched across cells.

</Callout>

</Mistakes>
