<Why>

Every number a DFT calculation gives you, a lattice constant, a formation energy, a band gap, depends on one choice: the approximation to the <Term id="exchange-correlation">exchange-correlation</Term> energy. In Kohn-Sham DFT it is the only term that is not known exactly <Cite id="S-012" />. Knowing what it contains helps you understand why different functionals give different answers, and why you must report which one you used.

</Why>

<Intuition>

Go back to the effective potential of the Kohn-Sham page. The nuclei's pull is exact. The Hartree repulsion is exact too, but it treats the electrons as a smooth cloud of charge, and that is too simple in two ways.

- **Exchange.** Electrons with the same spin cannot be in the same place, because of the Pauli principle. Each electron is surrounded by a small region where other same-spin electrons are rare. That lowers the repulsion compared to the smooth-cloud picture <Cite id="S-048" />.
- **Correlation.** Electrons also avoid each other simply because they repel, whatever their spin. The smooth cloud ignores this too <Cite id="S-048" />.

The smooth-cloud picture also lets each electron feel its own charge, a self-repulsion that is not physical; exchange is what cancels it. On top of this, the kinetic energy of the invented Kohn-Sham electrons is not quite the true kinetic energy, and the difference is folded into the same term. So "exchange-correlation" is the name for everything that the simple pieces leave out <Cite id="S-012" />.

Nobody knows the exact form of this term for real materials, so it is approximated. The two oldest and still most used families are:

- **LDA (local density approximation):** at each point, use the exchange-correlation energy of a uniform electron gas with the density found at that point.
- **GGA (generalized gradient approximation):** also use how fast the density changes there. PBE is the most common GGA in materials work <Cite id="S-013" />.

<Level min="intermediate">

Typical trends, as rules of thumb: LDA tends to overbind (lattice constants a little too small), PBE tends to underbind (a little too large), and both give band gaps that are too small <Cite id="S-049" />. Module 5 compares functionals and their costs.

</Level>

</Intuition>

<TheMath>

By definition,

$$E_{xc}[n] = \big(T[n] - T_s[n]\big) + \big(W[n] - E_H[n]\big),$$

the kinetic-energy difference between the interacting and the non-interacting electrons plus the electron-electron energy beyond the Hartree term <Cite id="S-012" />. Its potential is $v_{xc}(\mathbf{r}) = \delta E_{xc}/\delta n(\mathbf{r})$.

In the local density approximation,

$$E_{xc}^{\text{LDA}}[n] = \int n(\mathbf{r})\,\varepsilon_{xc}^{\text{unif}}\big(n(\mathbf{r})\big)\,d\mathbf{r},$$

with $\varepsilon_{xc}^{\text{unif}}(n)$ the exchange-correlation energy per electron of the uniform electron gas of density $n$. A GGA uses $\varepsilon_{xc}(n, |\nabla n|)$ <Cite id="S-049" /> <Cite id="S-013" />.

<Level min="pro">

The site's toy SCF lab on the next page uses Hartree-Fock, not DFT: exchange is treated exactly and correlation is left out entirely. For its two electrons the difference between the Hartree-Fock energy and an exact calculation on the same grid is the correlation energy of that model, which the lab shows when the loop has converged. That is a picture of what correlation is, not of what an approximate functional does.

</Level>

</TheMath>

<InVasp>

- VASP takes the functional from the POTCAR files (the `LEXCH` entry) unless you set `GGA` (or `METAGGA` or `XC`) in the INCAR. The VASP wiki page GGA states this default <Cite id="S-019" />. For PBE POTCARs that means PBE.
- Write the functional in every report or paper, and use the same one for every calculation you compare: energies from different functionals cannot be subtracted.

</InVasp>

<WhatChanges>

| If you change...                | ...then                                                                             |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| LDA to PBE                      | Lattice constants usually grow a little, binding usually weakens (rule of thumb)    |
| The functional between two runs | Their energies can no longer be compared                                            |
| Nothing (no GGA tag)            | VASP uses the functional the POTCAR was made for                                    |
| The functional for the band gap | Gaps from LDA or GGA are usually too small; better functionals cost more (module 5) |

Why this matters: it is the only approximation in Kohn-Sham DFT itself, so it decides how far your numbers are from the exact ones. What happens if you skip thinking about it: you may compare energies from runs that used different functionals.

</WhatChanges>

<Mistakes>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Thinking exchange-correlation is a small afterthought">

It is usually a modest part of the total energy, but energy differences, which are what you compare, are sensitive to it.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Mixing POTCARs and functionals by accident">

Without a GGA tag, VASP follows the POTCAR. Using LDA POTCARs in one run and PBE in another changes the functional silently.

</Callout>

<Callout variant="mistake" title="Thinking the toy lab shows a functional">

The SCF lab is Hartree-Fock for two electrons. It shows the loop, and what correlation is in that model, not how LDA or PBE behave.

</Callout>

</Mistakes>
